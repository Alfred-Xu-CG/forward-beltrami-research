## Result and boundary of the claim

The full Whitney 1-form mass matrix gives an explicit compatible edge/dual representation and reproduces the ordinary P1 conductivity operator to roundoff. Consequently **it does not improve the primal potential or nodal P1 conjugacy error over the same P1 problem**. Its discrete dual stream is integrable at harmonic interior vertices on a triangulated disk, while the elementwise vector field `A grad(u)` generally has jumps in normal flux across primal edges. These statements concern different representations and cannot be interchanged.

Code: `src/qcopt/forward/whitney_hodge.py`. Tests: `tests/test_phase5_whitney_hodge.py`. Reproducible experiment: `experiments/phase5/route3_whitney_hodge_benchmark.py`. Existing `anisotropic_hodge.py` was inspected but left unchanged: it is a four-direction scalar conductance representability audit, not an existing full Hodge implementation.

## Oriented construction and identities

Assume a nondegenerate conforming embedded triangular disk, with one connected face complex and facewise SPD tensors. Orient faces counterclockwise. Orient each global primal edge `e=(i,j)` from its smaller to larger vertex index unless explicitly reversed. Define

\[
(B_0 u)_e=u_j-u_i,\qquad (B_1)_{Te}=\pm1
\]

where the sign is positive if the edge follows the counterclockwise face boundary. Then `B1 B0=0` says that an exact potential difference has zero circulation around each triangle. **It is not flux conservation.**

Let `lambda_i` be local barycentric coordinates, `g_i=grad(lambda_i)`, and define the oriented Whitney basis

\[
w_{ij}=\lambda_i g_j-\lambda_j g_i.
\]

Reverse its sign when the local direction disagrees with the global edge. Its tangential integral is one on its own oriented edge and zero on the other local edges. For local potential differences `d_e`, the identity

\[
\sum_{e\subset T}d_e w_e=\nabla u_h|_T
\]

follows by expanding the three cyclic edges and using `sum lambda_i=1`, `sum g_i=0`. This is a pointwise identity on each face, not a quadrature approximation.

Assemble the full blocks

\[
(H_A^T)_{ef}=\int_T w_e^\mathsf T A_Tw_f\,dx,
\qquad L_A=B_0^\mathsf T H_A B_0.
\]

The implementation uses three edge-midpoint quadrature nodes of equal weight `|T|/3`, which integrates these degree-two integrands exactly for facewise constant tensors. Off-edge terms are retained. It explicitly uses `sym(A)` so the unconstrained tensor-argument VJP is symmetric; SPD checks apply to that symmetric part. It does not silently project an indefinite tensor to SPD.

Therefore

\[
u^\mathsf TL_Au=(B_0u)^\mathsf TH_A(B_0u)
=\sum_T|T|(\nabla u_T)^\mathsf TA_T\nabla u_T.
\]

The assembled matrix is exactly the usual P1 stiffness matrix. `H_A` is SPD on edge cochains: zero energy implies the Whitney field vanishes in every triangle, hence all tangential edge degrees of freedom vanish. `L_A` has the constants as its only nullspace on a connected mesh. After fixing a nonempty Dirichlet set it is SPD; on a disconnected mesh each component must meet that set. These are claims under the stated mesh and SPD assumptions, not universal guarantees for malformed connectivity. The implementation catches degenerate faces and edges incident to more than two triangles; it is not a geometric overlap or complete vertex-link validator.

An edge orientation change by a diagonal sign matrix `S` yields `B0'=S B0`, `H'=S H S`, so the potential operator is unchanged. Tests cover a face reversal and individual global edge reversals.

## What flux conservation and the dual stream mean

Define the **variational cochain**

\[
q=H_A B_0u.
\]

For interior vertices of the homogeneous Dirichlet PDE,

\[
(B_0^\mathsf Tq)_I=0.
\]

This is weak vertex flux balance, equivalent to the FEM equilibrium equation. Boundary rows generally contain reactions; they are not expected to vanish. It is separate from the identity `B1 B0=0`.

For interior primal edges, the corresponding dual edge goes from the right incident face to the left incident face. With this sign convention its incidence matrix is

\[
D=(B_1^\mathsf T)_{E_{\rm int},:}.
\]

The discrete dual stream equation is `D psi=q_int`. On a disk, the cycles of this interior dual graph are generated by dual cells surrounding interior primal vertices. Algebraically `B0_I^T D=0`, the connected dual graph gives `rank D=F-1`, and the disk Euler relation gives `E_int-V_int=F-1`; thus the weakly divergence-free cochains are dual-exact. The claim requires no interior source and trivial topology. Holes allow nonzero periods; sources obstruct the cycle equations. The code returns an LSQR residual on the supplied graph rather than asserting these hypotheses hold for arbitrary inputs.

The gauge fixes face 0 to zero. LSQR is a diagnostic and is not in the differentiable decoder path. Its numerical stopping error grows modestly with mesh size; the reported residual is `||D psi-q_int||_2/||q_int||_2`. The affine test `A=I, u=x` gives `psi_T=(centroid_T)_y+constant`, independently checking the stream sign.

This dual stream assigns numbers to **faces**. It is not a nodal P1 function `v_h`. Small dual residual does not establish `grad(v_h)=J A grad(u_h)` for a nodal map. Nor does it mean the original piecewise constant vector field has continuous physical normal traces. RT0 normal-flux conformity was not implemented here; Whitney cochain compatibility fully answers the algebraic flux question but leaves this stronger vector-field property absent.

## Differentiable Dirichlet solve

For fixed topology and boundary index set, solve

\[
L_{II}U_I=-L_{IB}b,\qquad U_B=b.
\]

The forward pass assembles element blocks through the Whitney construction into SciPy sparse COO/CSR and factors `L_II` once with SuperLU. Multiple scalar channels share that factor. No dense Hodge or dense stiffness matrix is required by the forward pass. Given output adjoint `g`, let `Lambda_B=0` and solve `L_II^T Lambda_I=g_I`. Then

\[
\frac{\partial\ell}{\partial A_T}
=-|T|\operatorname{sym}\left(\sum_r\nabla\Lambda_{T,r}\nabla U_{T,r}^\mathsf T\right),
\qquad
\frac{\partial\ell}{\partial b}=g_B-(L^\mathsf T\Lambda)_B.
\]

Any direct loss dependence on tensors or boundary parameters is separately handled by the surrounding Torch graph. The custom function supplies the solve-mediated term. Tests compare the custom VJP to independently differentiated dense Torch elimination and centered finite differences, including multiple right-hand sides. The tested maximum absolute directional FD discrepancy across the manufactured sweep is `5.89e-9` (N49 isotropic). There is no geometry derivative, source vector, double backward, GPU factorization, or batch of different matrices. These restrictions are explicit in the API.

Question: what does explicit Whitney structure preserve beyond ordinary P1, and what remains an approximation? Both operators use identical triangles, tensor, analytic Dirichlet data, and sparse solve accuracy. The independent baseline `assemble_facewise_conductivity` uses the existing `TriMesh.gradients` and direct local P1 energy assembly; it does not call the Whitney implementation.

On the unit square take `k=(1,0)`, `ell=J A k`, and determinant-one constant `A`. Then

\[
u=e^{k\cdot x}\cos(\ell\cdot x),\qquad
v=e^{k\cdot x}\sin(\ell\cdot x),\qquad
\nabla v=JA\nabla u.
\]

The cases are `A=I` and `A=[[2,.7],[.7,.745]]`. Both coordinates use their analytic Dirichlet values. A second independent baseline is the existing `integrate_stream_from_face_flux`: it averages face predictions along primal edges and integrates a spanning tree. ## Complexity, memory, timing, and reproduction

Mesh preprocessing, local Hodge assembly, and element matrix-free action cost O(F); for fixed RHS count they store O(V+E+F). Full sparse Hodge storage has at most nine local entries per triangle before coalescing. The actual solve factors the assembled potential matrix; it is not an iterative matrix-free solver. For regular 2D graphs with a suitable nested-dissection ordering, standard direct-solver estimates are O(V^1.5) factorization and O(V log V) factor storage/triangular work, but SuperLU's actual ordering/fill is what determines these runs. No universal factor-complexity bound is asserted for arbitrary meshes.

On the N25 anisotropic run, stored Hodge CSR is 111364 bytes, P1 CSR is 52628 bytes, sparse L/U arrays total 207472 bytes, and cached Whitney quadrature values total 165888 bytes. These counts omit other arrays, solver workspace, allocator overhead, and interpreter memory; they are **not peak memory measurements**. The sparse-factor byte count is taken from the independent P1 factor and is representative, not a measured accounting of the custom autograd context. The production API also retains geometry, sparse incidence arrays, the factor, matrix, and solution until backward finishes.

Cold forward (assembly plus factorization, two RHS) / backward was 5.92/1.25 ms at N25 anisotropic and 20.27/2.82 ms at N49. These are single executions on this CPU, not stabilized comparative benchmarks; geometry construction and diagnostics are excluded. They do not establish neural training performance.
