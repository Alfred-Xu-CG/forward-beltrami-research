# Twelve-hour extension: image registration with a certified dense P1 decoder

Status: ongoing research record, 2026-09-30 (China time). All numerical results in this document are **exploratory**. The seven anatomy-labelled pairs have been opened repeatedly during development; they are not a blind test set. Every saved result cited here is on D: under `D:\QC_optimization_data\digital_topology_wsi\ACROBAT_train_subset\scaleup102\imageonly_extension`.

## 1. Exact objective and what the layer returns

The requested object is a learnable map from an image-dependent latent state to a **piecewise-affine homeomorphism on a fixed, dense triangulation**, with a usable vector-Jacobian product (VJP). In this extension the reference domain is the closed unit square \(\Omega=[0,1]^2\), sampled at \(v_{ij}=(i/256,j/256)\), \(0\le i,j\le256\). Every one of the \(256^2\) squares has the same top-left-to-bottom-right diagonal, giving 131,072 reference triangles. A predicted vertex array \(Y\in\mathbb R^{257\times257\times2}\) defines a unique continuous P1 map \(F_Y\): on each triangle, barycentric interpolation of its three vertex values. The output is the **full 257² vertex table**, not merely a 65² control map or a dense image sampling grid.

For a real image pair, the network first receives an external orientation-preserving similarity affine \(A(x)=Mx+b\) inferred from image-only SuperPoint/SuperGlue correspondences and RANSAC. The certified residual \(F_Y:\Omega\to\Omega\) is composed as \(A\circ F_Y\). Conditional on this supplied affine, the neural branch is differentiable in its weights and image tensors; match-conditioned variants are additionally differentiable in their *selected continuous* match coordinates. Its output is P1 on the fixed triangulation. The *entire raw-image-to-deformation pipeline is not yet end-to-end differentiable*, because correspondence selection and RANSAC are discrete and external. Claiming otherwise would conflate two different notions of a differentiable layer.

## 2. Why every reported saved map is homeomorphic

For each reference vertex on the square boundary, the residual decoder keeps \(Y(v)=v\). Interior vertices are updated in four row/column parity classes: \((i\bmod2,j\bmod2)\in\{(0,0),(0,1),(1,0),(1,1)\}\). Within one class no two vertices share a triangle, so each affected oriented area is affine in that vertex's own displacement. At an interior vertex, let the current geometric central edges be

\[
e_x=\tfrac12(Y_{i+1,j}-Y_{i-1,j}),\qquad
e_y=\tfrac12(Y_{i,j+1}-Y_{i,j-1}).
\]

A latent \(z_i=(z_i^x,z_i^y)\) proposes \(r_i=8[e_x\tanh z_i^x+e_y\tanh z_i^y]\). For each incident oriented Q1-corner area \(a_k>0\), the area after moving only this vertex by \(t r_i\) is \(a_k+t\,d_k(r_i)\), where \(d_k\) is a known linear functional. The implemented smooth radial safety factor is based on the most restrictive negative area derivative and a local positive area budget (safety fraction 0.75; nominal minimum normalized Jacobian 0.05). There is **no global line search and no post-hoc fold repair**. The four classes are processed sequentially on the *current* deformed vertex table; the local edges are recomputed after earlier classes and passes. For an arbitrary proposed goal map, four passes steer toward that goal through this same safe operator.

This construction supplies a mathematical positive-area argument in exact arithmetic under finite inputs, positive initial map, fixed ordered boundary and the operator's stated area-budget conditions. The implementation also independently rechecks the **saved float32 bits**: all 262,144 Q1 corner determinants are strictly positive, the outer boundary equals the reference rectangle exactly, and the post-composed affine has positive determinant. The chosen-diagonal P1 triangles are therefore strictly positive, and the fixed-boundary planar disk map is globally homeomorphic, not merely locally fold-free. A nominal Jacobian floor is an implementation steering parameter, not a claim that every saved triangle satisfies the floor exactly after floating-point rounding. The certificate is geometry/topology, **not** evidence that anatomical landmarks are correctly registered.

## 3. Two different implementations: amortized neural output and per-pair multilevel fitting

### 3.1 Frozen image-only neural baseline and fast students

The common frozen baseline \(B\) uses a 512² fixed image and a moving image prewarped by the direct image-only affine. A 128² image feature extractor predicts coarse 17²/33²/65² safe levels, exactly refines to 257² and applies an independently proposed safe 257² head. The pretraining affines for the **new** baseline in this document were also generated directly from images; no DHR-derived initial affine or anatomical label is used by this baseline. Its frozen output, together with 88 image-feature channels and optionally a six-channel raster of machine-match offsets/density, enters a residual network. A four-pass residual decoder returns a 257² P1 map. One-shot inference does not run a per-pair optimizer.

The four student variants compared here have the same frozen baseline, 63 training and 18 case-ID-disjoint ACROBAT pseudo-teacher pairs, 1,000 AdamW minibatches, and four safe passes. The local trunk has three 3×3 convolutions; the global trunk downsamples 256→128→64→32→16 and upsamples with skips. A third design uses this global trunk to predict only interior latent controls at 17², 33² and 65², prolongs their zero-boundary fields, and safely steers the baseline toward their sum. It outputs the same 257² P1 map, but the learned geometric proposal has only 10,310 scalar controls instead of four independent full-grid latent fields. These are controlled architecture tests, not claims that U-Net or multiscale fields are new.

### 3.2 Pair-specific low-dimensional multilevel fitting

The strongest registration result so far in this extension is **not a one-pass learned network**. It freezes the same \(B\) and optimizes three two-component interior tensors \(c^{(17)},c^{(33)},c^{(65)}\), with

\[
N_c=2(15^2+31^2+63^2)=10{,}310.
\]

Let \(P_s\) zero-pad an interior \((s-2)^2\) tensor and bilinearly prolong its values to the 257² reference vertices. The unconstrained *goal*, not the output, is

\[
G_c=B+P_{17}c^{(17)}+P_{33}c^{(33)}+P_{65}c^{(65)}.
\]

The actual output is \(Y_c=T_4(B,G_c)\), where \(T_4\) means four sequential current-edge safety-steering passes. Consequently even if \(G_c\) folds, \(Y_c\) remains a certified fixed-grid P1 homeomorphism. This is a **forward sum of multiscale proposals followed by safe updates**; it neither inverts a 257² matrix nor composes separately triangulated maps and resamples them. Controls start at zero, so \(Y_0=B\). One diagnostic fit to a saved teacher map on ACROBAT case 233 reduced the baseline-to-teacher vertex RMSE from 0.003262 to 0.000764 unit after 100 steps; that supervised capacity check is not an anatomical result.

The actual landmark-blind registration objective uses a 256² eight-channel normalized self-similarity descriptor \(D\), a fixed tissue/texture mask \(w\), image-only aligned machine matches \((p_n,q_n)\), and a first-difference strain penalty:

\[
\begin{aligned}
L_{\rm image}(Y)&=\frac{\sum_{x,k}w(x)|D_F^k(x)-D_M^k(F_Y(x))|}
                         {8\sum_x w(x)+10^{-8}},\\
L_{\rm match}(Y)&=\frac1N\sum_{n=1}^N
  \left(\sqrt{\|512(F_Y(p_n)-q_n)\|_2^2+4^2}-4\right),\\
L_{\rm strain}(Y)&=\tfrac12\left[\operatorname{mean}\|256\Delta_x(Y-v)\|_2^2+
                              \operatorname{mean}\|256\Delta_y(Y-v)\|_2^2\right],\\
L(c)&=L_{\rm image}(Y_c)+0.05L_{\rm match}(Y_c)+L_{\rm strain}(Y_c).
\end{aligned}
\]

Here \(D_M\) is computed on the affine-prewarped moving canvas; \(q_n\) is correspondingly in that aligned frame. The image term samples the *P1 map* at 256² pixel-center queries, then bilinearly samples the descriptor. The match term is a smooth robust error in 512-canvas pixels. No anatomical landmark or DHR full field enters this objective. Adam learning rate is 0.002; the best iterate is selected by \(L\), never by anatomy. We predeclared 25 versus 100 steps to examine convergence. The same objective/step budgets were also used to compare zero-control starts with a neural-student map as a warm start.

## 4. Data, evaluation and provenance

The teacher-student training source is 81 ACROBAT image pairs, split deterministically by case ID into 63 train and 18 validation. The pseudo-teachers are 25-step per-pair multilevel fits from the frozen image-only baseline. All 81 teacher maps passed the saved topology certificate. Their supervision is *generated by the same images and machine matches*, not by anatomical landmark labels; teacher-map RMSE on the 18 validation cases measures amortization, not true registration accuracy.

Anatomy is measured on seven previously inspected development pairs: four stains (Cc10, CD31, Ki67, proSPC) against a single He image of one lung-lesion_3 specimen (80 corresponding points per stain); BIRL lesions (78), rat kidney (69), and HistoReg CD4/CD68 (77). The lung annotations were supplied at 50% image scale and converted to the provided 5% JPEG coordinates with the nominal center rule \(p_5=(p_{50}+0.5)/10-0.5\). Pixel-center conventions have subpixel uncertainty. For every fixed landmark \(x_n\), the predicted moving point is obtained by fixed-triangle barycentric interpolation of the 257² P1 map, application of the stored positive affine, and inversion of the recorded moving-canvas resize/padding. Target-registration error (TRE) is the mean Euclidean distance to its moving-image landmark in **native moving JPEG pixels**. The four lung errors can be averaged within that one common-scale specimen; the three BIRL/Histo errors must not be pooled into one physically meaningful mean because their source image scales differ.

These labels have been observed repeatedly in earlier rounds and in this extension. They support a feasibility diagnosis but not a clean generalization claim. The match archives do not all embed their affine metadata; the generation paths and baseline/teacher affine equality were checked, but machine-match frame consistency is not independently certified from each archive alone. External SuperGlue/RANSAC matching remains discrete and adds substantial upstream time.

### 5.1 What the network did and did not learn

On the 18 ACROBAT validation IDs, the frozen baseline's mean vertex RMSE to the 25-step pseudo-teacher was 0.003068 unit. A map-plus-image/match/strain-trained global full-grid student reduced that to 0.002828, while the global low-dimensional 17/33/65 student reached 0.002838. These are real case-disjoint improvements but recover only a small fraction of the per-pair teacher deformation. The global student's validation image term changed from 0.221995 to 0.219403 and robust match term from 0.941311 to 0.647425; its teacher's corresponding image and match terms were about 0.200981 and 0.057974. This large remaining gap is the core amortization bottleneck.

A controlled stronger machine-match training weight (0.01→0.1 with all else fixed) reduced the global student's validation match term from 0.647 to 0.614, but worsened its teacher-map RMSE from 0.002828 to 0.003018 and its lung anatomy mean from 12.543 to 12.888 px; Histo improved from 31.932 to 29.474 px. The same direction held for the low-dimensional head (validation match 0.642→0.601, teacher-map RMSE 0.002838→0.002960, lung 12.670→12.884, Histo 31.312→28.683). Thus simply increasing sparse-match supervision trades off tissue groups; it is not a universal fix.

Two additional global networks were trained **directly** against the pair-registration objective \(L_{\rm image}+0.05L_{\rm match}+L_{\rm strain}\) for 2,000 minibatches, with no teacher-map MSE term. On validation, full-grid and low-dimensional total objectives were 0.26457 and 0.26270, respectively, versus 0.26367 and 0.26218 for their 1,000-step pseudo-teacher-trained counterparts; both remained below the same frozen baseline 0.27889. Direct training therefore did not close the amortization gap. It also did not reliably improve anatomy: full-grid lung mean was 13.059 px (proSPC 15.209, worse than its affine 14.970); low-dimensional lung mean was 12.875, though Histo improved to 25.289. These negative or mixed outcomes are retained, not hidden by selecting the best pair.

### 5.2 Fast dual sparse-match multilevel proposal

A separate explicit proposal fits multilevel controls to machine-match residuals with a small dual system. Let \(\Phi\in\mathbb R^{N\times5155}\) contain the **exact fixed-diagonal 257-P1 interpolation weights** of the three prolonged interior hat bases at the \(N\) fixed match points, and let \(d_n=q_n-F_B(p_n)\). The two-component ridge solution is

\[
c=\Phi^T(\Phi\Phi^T+\lambda I_N)^{-1}d.
\]

The resulting proposed goal is passed through four current-edge safety steps, so solving the ridge problem does not assert that the unconstrained goal itself is homeomorphic. The 18 case-disjoint ACROBAT validation pairs had 18--94 matches each. For \(\lambda\in\{0.001,0.01,0.1,1,10\}\), the frozen baseline's teacher-map RMSE was 0.003068 unit; the dual method yielded 0.002715, 0.002715, 0.002718, 0.002775, and 0.002966. When 20% of matches were withheld from each fit, the baseline robust held-out match loss averaged 1.055 canvas pixels; the five dual values were 0.881, 0.881, 0.881, 0.906 and 1.004. This small-system method fitted the pseudo-teacher map at least as well as the tested amortized networks on that metric, but the validation teacher is not an anatomical ground truth.

All five ridges were applied to all seven already opened anatomy pairs, avoiding a landmark-selected ridge. Their lung mean TREs were 12.664, 12.662, 12.646, 12.624 and 12.756 px; Histo TREs were 30.090, 30.036, 29.595, 28.243 and 30.472 px. The dual method beat the initial affine on every pair at each ridge but did not approach the 25-step image-driven optimizer's 12.345 lung mean or 23.228 Histo. The 35 maps all passed saved exact Q1 certification and had finite match-coordinate VJPs. The fast branch is therefore a plausible **differentiable sparse-match initialization layer**, not a demonstrated one-shot solution to complex cross-stain registration. A 10-step image fit initialized from the validation-favoured \(\lambda=0.1\) dual map yielded lung mean 12.399, kidney 8.348 and Histo 24.941 px, versus 12.370/8.470/28.101 for a 10-step fit from the frozen map. Again, gains are tissue-dependent.

### 5.3 Independently checked ridge-goal/safety-pass gap

An independent check of the seven ridge-0.1 maps found exact agreement with the stated fixed-diagonal P1 design (including boundary/diagonal queries), positive exact saved Q1 signs on every map, positive post-affines, and anatomical TRE recomputation discrepancies below 1.3e-13 px. It also identified an important distinction: the ridge formula fits an unconstrained *goal*, whereas the final safe map is different. Mean machine-match residuals of baseline/goal/four-pass final (512-canvas pixels) were Cc10 3.863/0.338/1.493 and Histo 2.387/0.201/0.746; each other pair likewise had a higher final residual than its goal. The VJP is with respect to selected source and target match coordinates. Discrete matcher assignment, initial affine and baseline network are outside this graph.

We then fixed ridge 0.1 and all inputs, changing only safe passes from 4 to 8 and 16 on Cc10 and Histo. Cc10 final match residual fell from 1.493 to 0.868 and 0.512 canvas pixels, yet native landmark TRE moved from 10.362 to 10.386 and 10.404 px (slightly worse). Histo residual fell from 0.746 to 0.436 and 0.277 pixels, while TRE moved from 29.595 to 29.886 and 29.990 px (worse). All new saved maps passed exact certification. Safety attenuation is real, but forcing the map closer to these matches did not improve anatomy on these repeatedly viewed development pairs. The gap cannot be blamed solely on too few safe passes: the correspondence evidence and loss also need improvement. Separate cold CLI processes measured Cc10 8-pass 0.277/0.244 s forward/backward and 16-pass 0.350/0.403 s; these cold-start timings are not directly comparable with warm 4-pass timings.

The dual layer now has boundary/diagonal interpolation tests, a double-precision finite-difference target-coordinate VJP check, and nonfinite-target rejection. These strengthen implementation evidence but do not prove smooth gradients at every safety threshold. The probe source is tools/digital_dual_pass_probe.py and its raw result is on D: in the extension output directory.

### 5.4 Leave-one-out confidence: tested and not selected

We tested whether a match's inconsistency with the *other* matches predicts its reliability. With \(G=\Phi\Phi^T\), \(S=G+\lambda I\), \(\alpha=S^{-1}d\), and \(s_{ii}=(S^{-1})_{ii}\), the ridge leave-one-out displacement error is \(e_i=\alpha_i/s_{ii}\). A direct seven-row test recomputed each fit with row \(i\) omitted and matched this identity to numerical precision. We set a smooth weight \(w_i=[1+(512\|e_i\|/\tau)^2]^{-1}\) and solved the weighted ridge problem before the same four safety passes. This remained a finite-VJP, valid-map layer in the implemented test.

On the 18 case-disjoint ACROBAT validation pairs, fixed ridge 0.1 with no reweighting gave teacher-map RMSE 0.0027183 unit and 20%-held-out match robust loss 0.8807 canvas pixels. Scales \(\tau=2,4,8\) px yielded RMSE 0.0027583, 0.0027301, 0.0027213 and held-out loss 0.8976, 0.8854, 0.8819, respectively. All were **worse** than the unweighted comparator on both predeclared diagnostics. We therefore did not use anatomy labels to pick a weight scale and did not extend this variant to seven anatomy pairs. This negative result does not prove every confidence model fails; it shows that this particular spatial-LOO proxy did not resolve the evidence problem.

### 5.5 Explicit image-force cue: first fast near-teacher result

The first-order image-force cue was constructed at the frozen safe map from the fixed and warped moving eight-channel self-similarity descriptors. We centrally differenced the moving descriptor, bilinearly sampled those two spatial derivatives through the fixed-grid P1 map, multiplied them by a smooth signed descriptor residual, and averaged over the eight channels. The two normalized force components, their two seven-pixel smoothed versions, mismatch magnitude and validity mask formed six additional channels beside the existing six Gaussian match-raster channels. These channels are **not** claimed to be the exact structural-loss gradient. Crucially, they are themselves ordinary first-order differentiable functions of images and the frozen map; an explicit finite-difference image-input test passed. We avoided differentiating through an autograd gradient because the installed grid-sampling backward has no second derivative.

Only the evidence channels changed relative to the matched global U-Net student: same 63/18 ACROBAT case-ID split, 25-step image-only pseudo-teachers, frozen baseline, 1000 minibatches of size two, training losses and four-pass safe decoder. The 18 validation IDs improved from teacher-map RMSE 0.0028283 to **0.0026635** normalized units, image loss 0.21940 to **0.20758**, and robust match loss 0.64743 to **0.60143** canvas pixels. Strain rose from 0.01189 to 0.01671, so the better image and match fit carries more deformation; this tradeoff needs explicit monitoring.

On the seven already viewed anatomy pairs, the one-shot image-force student scored Cc10 10.058, CD31 7.185, Ki67 18.135, proSPC 14.160, lesions 7.291, kidney 8.570 and Histo **28.073** native moving-image pixels. The four-pair lung mean was **12.384**. This improves all seven initial affines and all seven prior matched global-student results (10.144, 7.302, 18.251, 14.477, 7.486, 9.332, 31.932). It almost reaches, but does not surpass, the 25-step per-pair lung mean 12.345; it remains worse on Histo than 25-step fitting's 23.228 px. None of these seven pairs is a fresh blind test, and this does not establish large-scale unseen pathology generalization.

All seven saved float32 maps had exactly 262,144/262,144 positive Q1 corners, exact fixed rectangle boundary and positive post-affine, with finite parameter/selected-match VJP. Forward time was about 68 ms per 257² map; observed full forward-plus-VJP ranged about 178--302 ms across pairs/runs, not including external affine/matching or image file decoding. Cc10 peak PyTorch CUDA allocation was 175,582,208 bytes forward and 1,303,437,824 bytes for forward-plus-VJP (about 167 MiB and 1.21 GiB). The matched earlier global student's Cc10 forward was 48 ms, with 167,195,648 bytes forward and 1,264,385,536 bytes VJP peak, so the better force-conditioned accuracy costs extra latency and modest extra allocated memory. The independent checker recomputed all seven landmark scores to below 3e-14 px discrepancy and all seven exact saved topology signs; it additionally found nonzero finite fixed/moving image-pixel VJPs through the full inference chain and one moving-image directional finite difference within about 3.5% at the tested step. The archived finite-VJP flag itself checks model parameters and selected match coordinates, not image pixels. Training caches force channels under no-grad and updates only the residual student; inference differentiates through force, the frozen base and one-head modules. The 12-channel force model also has 1,728 more parameters than the six-channel comparator, so the observed gain is not yet a strictly equal-capacity ablation.

The same force cue was also tested with the pre-existing 17/33/65-control multilevel U-Net, holding its six-channel comparator's training setup fixed. On the same 18 validation cases, its teacher-map RMSE changed from 0.0028384 to **0.0026375**, image term 0.21919 to **0.21036**, and match term 0.64153 to **0.62320**; strain rose from 0.01092 to 0.01385. Seven-case native TRE was Cc10 10.143, CD31 7.120, Ki67 18.198, proSPC 14.334, lesions **7.183**, kidney 8.720 and Histo **25.607** px. The four-lung mean was 12.449. Thus low-dimensional force conditioning is slightly worse than the full-grid force network's 12.384 lung mean but distinctly better on Histo (25.607 vs 28.073) and lesions. It also remains short of Histo's 23.228-px 25-step pair fit. All seven saved maps passed the exact certificate and finite full parameter/selected-match VJP check. Forward was roughly 47--49 ms; Histo full forward-plus-VJP was 156 ms and PyTorch VJP peak 854,866,432 bytes (about 815 MiB), excluding matcher/affine generation. Which branch generalizes best remains unresolved because the anatomy pairs are reused development cases.

The matched global force network trained for 2000 rather than 1000 minibatches (identical first 1000 minibatches and all settings) improved the 18-case validation teacher-map RMSE 0.0026635→0.0026237, image loss 0.20758→0.20642 and robust match 0.60143→0.59523, while raising strain 0.01671→0.01982. Its seven viewed anatomy TREs were 10.263/7.144/17.891/14.655/7.559/8.857/28.718 px, with lung mean 12.488. Thus additional training improved all three unlabeled validation proxies but **worsened** five of seven anatomy pairs relative to the 1000-step force student; Ki67 and CD31 improved. This reinforces that teacher-map and self-similarity gains are not a universally faithful anatomical model-selection criterion. Both checkpoints are retained; no best-anatomy cherry-picking is presented as independent evidence.

The seven selected-machine-match archives lack affine-frame metadata. The saved maps' post-affines match their direct image-only initial affine archives bitwise, but the code can only check match shapes and finiteness in these cases; it cannot *machine-certify* that source/target match coordinates were produced in that affine frame. This is an input-provenance limitation, not a topology or gradient failure.

A targeted provenance normalization subsequently checked each of the seven original match JSON files: its named source affine basename matches the corresponding direct image-only affine, and its float32 point arrays equal the compact match archive exactly; each affine equals the saved force-student map's post-affine bitwise. It wrote new match archives with explicit affine metadata, retaining the originals, under the D: extension output directory. Re-running Cc10 with the metadata-bearing archive set the inference frame-check flag to true and reproduced the prior map vertices bitwise. This establishes consistency among the available provenance records and files; it cannot independently prove the historical matcher actually prewarped with the affine named in its own JSON report.

To control the extra twelve-channel stem width noted by the independent checker, we trained a same-shape, same-seed 1000-minibatch global U-Net with its last six evidence channels identically zero. Its 18-case teacher-map RMSE/image/match were 0.0028494/0.21884/0.66284, versus 0.0026635/0.20758/0.60143 for the force channels, under identical teachers, split and loss weights. On seven viewed anatomy pairs, this zero-channel control scored 10.476/7.521/18.368/14.586/7.233/9.690/28.788 px (lung mean 12.738). The force model improved six of seven pairs relative to this control; lesions were slightly worse (7.291 vs 7.233). Thus the main gain is not explained by input-tensor shape or the extra stem arithmetic alone. Because zero channels give their associated weights no useful gradient, this is a shape/compute control, not a perfectly equal *effective parameter* comparison. A randomized or learned non-force twelve-channel control remains untested.

### 5.6 Image-only force at inference: meaningful but limited one-shot registration

The force-only student uses the same image-only-affine-prewarped fixed and moving tensors, frozen baseline and 257-square four-pass safety decoder, but gives the residual U-Net **six force channels and no machine-match raster**. At inference, `matches_path=None` is enforced and no selected correspondence coordinates enter the residual graph. Offline 25-step teachers and the auxiliary training match loss still use machine correspondences, and the external initial affine is itself obtained by SuperGlue/RANSAC. Thus “force-only” means *no matches as residual inference features*, not a match-free or differentiable raw-image alignment pipeline.

For clarity, let the fixed eight-channel descriptor be \(D_F^k(x)\), the moving descriptor already sampled at the frozen P1 map \(B\) be \(D_M^k(B(x))\), and the valid-image mask be \(w(x)\). Set \(r_k(x)=D_F^k(x)-D_M^k(B(x))\), \(s_k=r_k/\sqrt{r_k^2+0.05^2}\). With \(\partial_x^c D_M,\partial_y^c D_M\) the central differences on the 256-square descriptor array, the two unnormalized cues are

\[
g_a(x)=-\frac{w(x)}8\sum_{k=1}^8 s_k(x)
     [\partial_a^cD_M^k](B(x)),\qquad a\in\{x,y\}.
\]

Central differences are scaled to unit-square coordinates; their moving-image values are bilinearly sampled at the P1-deformed 256-square pixel centers. The implementation divides \((g_x,g_y)\) by three times its per-image root-mean-square magnitude plus \(10^{-5}\), applies `tanh`, forms a 7×7 local average of both normalized components, then concatenates mean absolute descriptor mismatch and \(w\). These six channels are a **learned decision cue**, not the exact derivative of the absolute-value structural objective. At each of four passes the network predicts a new two-component latent field; the current-edge safety operator changes the same 257² vertex table. The output is still a single fixed-triangulation P1 map.

With 1000 minibatches and unchanged balanced loss, its 18 case-ID-held-out ACROBAT means were teacher-map RMSE **0.0028099** normalized units, descriptor-image loss **0.20510**, machine-match robust loss **0.72135** canvas pixels (evaluation only at inference) and strain **0.02081**. The corresponding match-plus-force U-Net was 0.0026635/0.20758/0.60143/0.01671. Therefore removing match evidence improved the image proxy a little, but made teacher imitation, held-out match fit and strain worse; these metrics alone do not rank anatomical performance.

On *all seven already viewed* real anatomy pairs, the force-only map scored Cc10 **10.458**, CD31 **6.916**, Ki67 **18.555**, proSPC **14.735**, lesions **7.460**, kidney **8.894** and Histo **26.105** native moving-JPEG pixels. Lung four-pair mean was **12.666**. The independent checker reconstructed the seven scores from raw CSVs to within \(1.4\times10^{-14}\) px, verified seven exact saved Q1 certificates (262,144 positive corners each, fixed boundary and positive bitwise-matched affines), and found nonzero finite fixed- and moving-image-pixel gradients of a **map-output scalar**, not merely an image-loss direct path. A moving-image directional finite difference differed from the analytic derivative by about 7.8% at one float32 step; this is directional evidence, not a universal gradient theorem. The seven inference reports all record `matches: null` and `match_count: 0`.

All fourteen new maps passed the exact saved Q1 certificate and finite VJP checks. This controlled negative result locates a **data/objective bottleneck**: minimizing the current descriptor similarity more aggressively can produce valid homeomorphisms that are anatomically less accurate. Topological correctness does not make the image loss anatomically calibrated.

### 5.7 Recomputing force at the current safe map: main neural candidate

Static force becomes stale as the map moves. We therefore kept the same six-channel U-Net, four 257-square F1 passes, 63/18 ACROBAT case split, 1000 minibatches, teachers, random seed and balanced loss, but changed the evidence evaluation. Write \(Y^{(0)}=B\), \(C(Y)\) for the 256-square centers of the P1 map, and \(g(Y)\) for the six-channel force construction in §5.6 with \(B\) replaced by \(Y\). For pass \(k=0,1,2,3\), the decoder computes

\[
z^{(k)}=H_{\theta,k}\!\left(\operatorname{concat}
  [\psi_B,\ g(Y^{(k)}),\ C(Y^{(k)})-C(B)]\right),\qquad
Y^{(k+1)}=S\bigl(Y^{(k)},z^{(k)}\bigr).
\]

Here \(\psi_B\) is the **fixed** 88-channel image feature tensor computed once at \(B\), \(H_{\theta,k}\) shares its U-Net trunk across passes but has a separate two-channel head for each pass, and \(S\) is the current-edge, four-color analytic area-safe update of §2. Thus each pass resamples only the moving descriptor and its two central differences at the *current* map and refreshes the two center-displacement feedback channels; it does **not** recompute all 88 channels, take an explicit gradient-descent step, solve a mesh-wide system, compose four independently triangulated maps, or resample the final map. The same 257² vertex table is updated four times and interpreted as one final fixed-grid P1 map. Differentiation through the four cue evaluations and safe updates uses ordinary first-order reverse-mode AD; branch boundaries make the map only almost-everywhere differentiable.

Against the static six-channel force-only control, the dynamic model improved **17/18** held-out teacher-map errors (mean 0.002810→**0.002676** unit), **18/18** held-out image losses (mean 0.20510→**0.19865**), and **15/18** machine-match robust losses (mean 0.72135→**0.65844** canvas px). Mean strain worsened 0.02081→0.02323 (17/18 cases higher). These are *paired* case-ID comparisons with exactly the same architecture size and input-channel count; unlike the twelve-channel force comparison, added parameters cannot explain the change. The intervention changes both the feature values and the gradient path by recomputing them on each intermediate map.

On all seven already viewed anatomy pairs, dynamic no-match-raster inference scored Cc10 **10.184**, CD31 **6.580**, Ki67 **18.313**, proSPC **14.277**, lesions **7.475**, kidney **7.959** and Histo **23.424** native moving-JPEG pixels. The lung mean was **12.338**. Relative to static force-only, six of seven improved; lesions worsened slightly by 0.016 px. These are below the initial affine on all seven pairs. The one-shot 12.338 lung mean is close to the *different* 25-step pair-specific fit's 12.345; Histo 23.424 is close to that fit's 23.228, and kidney 7.959 is better than its 8.282. This comparison is exploratory, **not** a claim that the neural model matches an optimizer on independent pathology patients. The initial affine remains external matching preprocessing, and the offline teacher/match training loss still relies on correspondences.

Batch-one GPU median forward was **58–61 ms** for a 257² map; full forward-plus-VJP was approximately **192–338 ms** across the seven pairs, with the slowest first Cc10 measurement reflecting initialization. Cc10 forward peak PyTorch allocation was 168,438,784 bytes (~161 MiB) and forward-plus-VJP peak 1,532,379,136 bytes (~1.43 GiB). Dynamic force is therefore only about 9 ms slower forward than the static force-only model in these measurements, but its saved backward graph costs additional memory. These figures exclude image decoding, external affine estimation, serialization and exact post-save certification.

The independent checker recomputed all seven scores directly from raw CSVs (discrepancy ≤1e−13 px), verified all seven saved binary32 maps have 262,144/262,144 positive Q1 corner signs with exact fixed boundaries and positive, bitwise-matched post-affines, and instrumented the actual Cc10 checkpoint: all four cue calls used different current maps and produced changing force features. A map-output scalar (not an image-loss direct path) had nonzero finite fixed/moving image-pixel VJPs. For one representative moving-image direction, the analytic derivative 0.135900 was approached by central finite differences 0.127777, 0.131607 and 0.133991 as the perturbation decreased; with small input dither another direction differed by 0.57%. Another direction crossed a piecewise branch and did **not** show convergence, so we claim an almost-everywhere usable first-order VJP, not smoothness or reliable second derivatives everywhere.

Improved anatomy came with a local distortion tradeoff. On Histo, the minimum normalized Q1 corner determinant fell from static force-only 0.1871 to dynamic **0.0564**, with 29 of 262,144 corners below 0.1 (0.011%); CD31 had 24 below 0.1, whereas the other five dynamic maps had none. Every corner remained strictly positive and each saved map passed the exact topological certificate. Distortion and topology must remain separate conclusions.

### 5.8 Stronger strain weight: a safer operating point on viewed cases

We retrained the same dynamic-force architecture from the same initialization, with the same 63/18 split, seed, 1000 minibatches, teachers and map/image/match weights, changing only the first-gradient strain coefficient **0.02→0.2**. On the 18 held-out ACROBAT IDs, mean strain improved **0.02323→0.01487**. Teacher-map RMSE worsened 0.002676→0.002710, image loss 0.19865→0.20454, and robust machine-match loss 0.65844→0.67452. However all four metrics were still better than the *static force-only* control (0.02081 strain, 0.002810 RMSE, 0.20510 image, 0.72135 match). The two dynamic settings are an explicit quality/distortion tradeoff, not a hidden best-of-seven selection.

All seven already viewed anatomy scores for the stronger-strain dynamic model were Cc10 **10.104**, CD31 **6.494**, Ki67 **18.371**, proSPC **14.380**, lesions **7.675**, kidney **7.942** and Histo **22.075** native moving-JPEG pixels; lung mean **12.337**. Compared with coefficient 0.02, Histo improved 1.349 px and kidney/CD31/Cc10 slightly improved, while Ki67, proSPC and lesions slightly worsened; the lung mean changed by only 0.001 px. All seven beat their direct initial affines. On Histo the minimum normalized Q1 corner determinant rose **0.0564→0.2672** and the number of corners below 0.1 fell **29→0**; on CD31 the minimum rose **0.0609→0.1058** and low-corner count **24→0**. All seven stronger-strain maps had zero corners below 0.1, in addition to passing the exact binary32 topology certificate. An independent checker recomputed all seven native-JPEG TREs from raw labels within \(2\times10^{-14}\) px and verified Q1/boundary/affine signs. This is a more attractive *development-case operating point*, not proof that coefficient 0.2 generalizes better to unseen anatomy.

### 5.9 Dynamic force without teacher-map or match losses

To separate the current-map feedback mechanism from offline match-optimized teacher imitation, we trained a third six-channel dynamic-force residual head with **map weight 0, match weight 0, image weight 1, strain weight 1**, holding the frozen baseline, 63/18 cohort, seed and 1000-minibatch budget fixed. The training loop still loads teacher and match files to define eligible cases, compute diagnostic quantities and ensure at least 16 available matches; the zero-weight terms send no gradient to the student when their finite diagnostic values are multiplied by zero. Inference again has no selected correspondence inputs. Therefore this is *match-loss-free residual training*, **not** a fully match-free data cohort, frozen representation or raw-image pipeline.

The 18 held-out ACROBAT means were image loss **0.19891**, strain **0.01618**, teacher-map RMSE **0.002550** and robust machine-match loss **0.6630** canvas pixels. These surprising teacher/match diagnostic improvements despite zero corresponding loss coefficients should not be interpreted as evidence that anatomy is optimized by the image descriptor. On seven viewed pairs the native TRE was Cc10 **10.314**, CD31 **6.782**, Ki67 **18.493**, proSPC **14.488**, lesions **7.368**, kidney **8.588** and Histo **22.201** px, lung mean **12.519**. All seven improved over their direct initial affine. The result is markedly better on Histo than static force-only with the same no-teacher/no-match-loss intent (29.510 px), though the older static control used a weaker strain coefficient 0.02, so that particular difference cannot be attributed to dynamism alone. It is slightly worse in lung mean than the balanced dynamic strain-0.2 model but nearly equal on Histo. All seven saved maps passed exact Q1/boundary/affine certification and had finite VJPs. An independent checker recomputed all seven raw-landmark scores to within \(8\times10^{-15}\) px and confirmed the zero teacher/match gradient coefficients in the actual checkpoint/training loop.

Distortion remains the limiting factor for this loss-only arm: Histo's minimum normalized Q1 corner determinant was **0.04999**, with 114/262,144 corners below 0.1; CD31 minimum was 0.06488 with 24 below 0.1. These are strictly positive and topologically valid, but worse local thickness than the balanced dynamic strain-0.2 model's zero sub-0.1 corners across seven cases. Increasing this arm's strain weight or using a feature objective better aligned with anatomy could be tested in a future frozen protocol, but repeatedly selecting by these seven labels would overstate evidence.

### 5.13 Safe residual-amplitude ablation: more movement is not a cure

To test whether the learned four-pass residual was merely too small, a card in `TOPOLOGY.md` predeclared gains \(g=1,2,4\) multiplying each pass's latent logits **before** the same current-edge topology-safe update. The first zero-teacher-map-gradient run retained the same 12-channel dynamic architecture, 63/18 split, 1000 minibatches and starting checkpoint, but used weights `(map,image,machine-match,strain)=(0,1,0.1,1)` instead of `(1000,0.1,0.01,0.2)`. All seven saved anatomy maps of this multi-coefficient run passed exact Q1/boundary checks and finite VJP reporting. The 3000-step overfit result in §5.16 raised a separate small-data hypothesis. ### 5.19 Analytic residual-match feedback pass: topology succeeds, proxy conflict remains

As a non-learned forward alternative to another U-Net or a large linear solve, `tools/digital_match_feedback257.py` computes the mismatch (e_k=t_k-u(s_k)) of the *current* 12-channel neural map at each selected machine match, Gaussian-averages these residuals over the 255² interior vertices with fixed (\sigma=.04,\tau=.05\), expresses the desired displacement in each current local edge basis, and passes the implied logits through **one more** certified F1 update. The Gaussian field by itself is not topologically safe; the F1 operator is. The selected-match coordinates, image-pixel path and neural parameters remain in the differentiable graph, but external matcher/RANSAC selection does not. A double-precision single-correspondence test checked a nonzero finite VJP and a target-coordinate directional finite difference; it is not a full-system gradient proof.

We fixed gain one and ran *all* 18 validation and eight confirmation cases before any anatomical scoring. Relative to the same saved 12-channel neural model without this pass, mean Euclidean machine-match residual fell **1.78790→1.51844 px** on validation and **1.80125→1.53054 px** on confirmation, improving **26/26** pairs. Yet the image proxy worsened **0.204497→0.206083** and **0.185083→0.186823**, also on **26/26** pairs. All 26 newly saved maps remained exact-Q1/boundary valid with positive unchanged post-affines and finite reported VJPs, but validation's minimum normalized Q1 corner fell **0.103323→0.094109**, acquiring three sub-0.1 corners; confirmation's minimum fell **0.165818→0.157097**. Mean neural forward rose **62.1→89.3 ms** on validation and **61.7→65.9 ms** on confirmation; recorded maximum PyTorch VJP allocation rose from roughly **1.55 to 1.64 GB**. Independent recalculation from original selected-match NPZs and saved P1 maps confirmed all 26 match-error decreases, all 26 image-proxy increases and exact signs. Because the predeclared gate required acceptable image **and** match evidence and geometry, this variant was **not** scored on already viewed anatomy labels. It shows that even a mathematically sound, fast, topology-safe forward correction can optimize the *wrong correspondence proxy* for the broader registration goal.

### 5.20 Direct gradient check on the production map output

The earlier finite-difference test involved only a reduced two-pass synthetic decoder; moreover, a finite gradient of the production *image loss* with respect to image pixels does not by itself prove a gradient through the **map**, because the loss also depends directly on image descriptors. We therefore added an optional map-output probe to the unchanged predictor. It holds the selected matches, matcher decisions and affine fixed, forms a deterministic Gaussian-weighted **linear functional of the predicted vertex displacement** near the first selected machine source, and compares its reverse-mode derivative against central differences for three independent input directions: a localized fixed-image-pixel pattern, a localized moving-image-pixel pattern, and the first selected match target's horizontal coordinate. No anatomy labels enter this probe.

The probe used two previously chosen ACROBAT pairs: validation case 657 and confirmation case 216. It cast the same trained float32 weights to float64 for numerical differencing; this tests the complete base→one-head→four-pass architecture at double precision, **not bitwise the float32 deployed function**. All six directional automatic derivatives were nonzero. At the most favourable tested small step, relative differences \(|D_{\rm FD}-D_{\rm AD}|/\max(|D_{\rm FD}|,|D_{\rm AD}|)\) were:

| Case | Fixed pixels | Moving pixels | Selected target x |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 657 | 1.079% | 0.0967% | 0.0090% |
| 216 | 0.2913% | 0.0904% | 0.1384% |

The two float64 saved maps passed exact binary Q1/boundary checks at all 262,144 corners, and every plus/minus perturbation map passed the in-memory topology check. The final descriptor mask changed on none of these local perturbations. The numbers are **not** uniformly tiny: case 657's fixed-pixel discrepancy remained about 1.08% even at \(10^{-7}\), and larger moving-pixel steps could differ by 22.5%. The descriptor uses `amin`, absolute values and threshold masks, while the safe operator uses min/max; local nonsmooth changes and finite-difference step choice can matter even without a final mask change. An independent checker confirmed that the scalar has no direct image-loss path, checked both JSONs and exact saved-map certificates, and limited the conclusion to **local evidence for a usable map-output VJP**. This does not verify arbitrary image pixels, selected-match sources, every network parameter, the external matcher/RANSAC or affine estimation. The reproducible reports are D-drive `imageonly_extension/phase6_full_map_vjp_probe{657,216}_float64_micro.json`.

### 5.21 Four triangulation-preserving spatial symmetries: mixed validation

To test whether 63 pairs lacked simple orientation diversity, we made four **training-only**, common-coordinate augmentations: identity, 180° rotation, main-diagonal reflection and anti-diagonal reflection. Both images, the frozen starting map, pseudo-teacher and both coordinates of every selected machine match are transformed by the same square isometry \(\rho\); the map target is \(u^\rho=\rho\circ u\circ\rho^{-1}\). Unlike a 90° rotation of a single-diagonal square triangulation, these four transformations preserve its diagonal and therefore represent the conjugated **same P1 map exactly** on the same vertex grid. A five-by-five nontrivial-map test checked pointwise P1 conjugation away from vertices, Q1 positivity, image involutions and map involutions. The validation images were not augmented. The default one-variant code path retains the prior initial 18 validation rows *exactly*, and the four-variant run's initial rows also matched the control exactly.

With the otherwise unchanged 12-channel dynamic model, 63/18 split, 1000 minibatches, seed, pseudo-teachers and weights `(1000,0.1,0.01,0.2)`, the four-symmetry run gave these 18-case means:

|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Pseudo-teacher map RMSE, unit-square | 0.00250446 | 0.00250942 | 9 |

### 5.24 Cell-to-vertex readout: exact rank, ill-conditioning, and a controlled negative architecture test

The retained recurrent head computes two logits on 256² cells, uses PyTorch bilinear `align_corners=False` interpolation to 257² vertices, crops the 255² interior, and then invokes the same current-edge safe map update. The one-dimensional readout matrix \(U\in\mathbb R^{255\times256}\) is mathematically full row rank, so its two-dimensional tensor product is locally onto interior logit fields if the pre-interpolation cell values are free. However, a constructive **left** vector gives \(\sigma_{\min}(U)\le5.88125\times10^{-76}\). This is a severe conditioning issue for a particular alternating mode, not proof of inadequate capacity for pathology registration. The full derivation, including why a right-vector near-kernel argument would be invalid, is in `CURRENT_LEARNED_LAYER_FORMULATION.md` §3.1.

We tested relevance to the actual supervision before changing the network. `tools/digital_readout_capacity_audit.py` projected the **teacher minus frozen-start** 255² interior vertex-displacement field for each of the retained model's 18 validation IDs onto the binary64-computed near-null left mode along either spatial axis. The orthogonal projected energy divided by total squared interior displacement energy averaged **0.00018345** (0.0183%) and reached at most **0.00051206** (0.0512%). The diagnostic is linearized and concerns these pseudo-teacher targets, not anatomy or the complete nonlinear safe decoder; nonetheless, it suggests this single ill-conditioned subspace carries very little of the current target motion. The per-case fractions and map-gap RMS values are in `phase6_readout_capacity_val18.json` on D:.

We then added an optional, zero-initialized **additive** `ConvTranspose2d(32,2,kernel=2,stride=1)` readout at each of the four passes. It maps the same 256² hidden U-Net features directly to 257² logits and adds them to the unchanged bilinear logits; the current-edge safety operator and topology guarantee are untouched. This adds **1,032** trainable parameters across four heads, not a second map-composition or fold repair. A unit test verifies identical initial map outputs and nonzero gradients through the added head; the full training reports' 18 initial validation rows also matched exactly. The direct-head checkpoint's four new weight norms were 0.1272/0.1263/0.1206/0.1229, so the branch did learn rather than remain zero.

With the same 63/18 split, seed, 1000 minibatches of two, 25-step pseudo-teachers, external frozen starts, image/match evidence, and loss weights `(1000,0.1,0.01,0.2)`, the old→direct-head validation means were: teacher-map RMSE **0.00250446→0.00257391** (4/18 cases improved), image proxy **0.20449686→0.20480669** (4/18), robust match **0.53880084→0.54283056** px (7/18), and strain **0.01469131→0.01472455** (7/18). Training time rose **202.39→213.09 s**; peak PyTorch allocation was **4,655,400,448→4,655,418,880 bytes**. This isolated architecture variant did not meet the predeclared coherent unlabeled-validation gate, so no reused-anatomy TRE was run. It remains possible that a different direct-vertex architecture or truly high-frequency target supervision helps, but this experiment does not support the readout conditioning as the present leading bottleneck. Checkpoint/report/log are `phase6_direct_vertex_residual_strain02_1000.{pt,json,log}` on D:.

### 5.26 Which part of match feedback conflicts with the image objective?

The analytic gain-1 match-feedback pass (§5.19) improved machine-match fit but worsened the image descriptor on 26/26 saved ACROBAT maps. To distinguish *raw objective-gradient opposition* from *the particular finite safe update*, `tools/digital_proxy_gradient_conflict.py` recomputed the same image loss and robust-match loss used during training at the unchanged retained 257² map. It differentiated each loss with respect to the **aligned 255² interior vertex table**, excluding the fixed boundary. This is not the VJP through network parameters, the external discrete matcher or the safe update's latent. The exact image values matched the production reports case by case; available match NPZs lack embedded affine metadata, so their aligned-frame provenance cannot be re-proved solely from those archives.

Among 18 case-disjoint validation and eight previously exposed confirmation images, only **3/26** image-versus-match interior-gradient dot products were negative. The mean cosine was **+0.00721**, nearly orthogonal in the full vertex space. The machine-match gradient touched a mean **163.4** of 65,025 interior vertices; only **1.66%** of image-gradient squared energy lay on that sparse support on average. Restricting the image gradient to that support raised the mean cosine to **+0.0654**, still weakly aligned rather than predominantly opposed. These normalized angles are not comparisons of gradient magnitudes: the losses have different units and denominators.

For the **actual saved** gain-1 feedback displacement \(\Delta u\), \(\langle\nabla L_{\rm image},\Delta u\rangle>0\) in **16/26** cases and \(\langle\nabla L_{\rm match},\Delta u\rangle<0\) in **26/26**. The measured finite image change was positive in **26/26**, while robust match loss fell in **26/26** (mean changes about **+0.001633** dimensionless image units and **−0.13275 px** robust-match units). In the ten cases where the local image dot product was negative, the complete finite step nevertheless worsened the image objective. The finite change minus its first-order linear prediction was positive in all 26 cases, but this is **not** a Hessian-curvature proof: for the already fixed displacement \(\Delta u\), descriptor interpolation, piecewise-absolute terms and P1 cell transitions can make the evaluated objective nonlinear or nonsmooth along the segment. The safety operator determines \(\Delta u\); it does not itself enter that Taylor remainder.

We also held the starting maps and selected matches fixed and varied only analytic feedback gain through \(0.05,0.1,0.25,0.5,1\). The image proxy worsened on **20/20/24/25/26 of 26** cases, respectively; robust match loss improved on **26/26** at every gain. At gain one the GPU-reconstructed maps equaled the previously saved feedback maps exactly; an independent CPU replay differed by at most one binary32 unit in the last place. The gain-sweep candidates passed a runtime float32 positive-corner check (minimum normalized corner at the five gains roughly **0.1039/0.1047/0.1089/0.1118/0.0941**), but were **not all saved and exact-binary certified**. This is a diagnostic, not a promoted registration layer or anatomy result. It rejects the simple story “the two original objectives always point opposite ways” and also shows that a small positive feedback gain does not generally remove their finite proxy tradeoff. Detailed 18/8 results are `phase6_proxy_gradient_gain_{val18,confirm8}_v2.json` on D:.

### 5.28 Is the learned increment genuinely non-affine?

The stronger anatomy result could still be a disguised second affine correction. To measure geometry without looking at annotations, `tools/digital_lung_all20_nonaffine.py` takes each **unchanged saved 257² composite vertex table**, projects it in float64 onto the three spatial basis fields (1,x,y) under equal vertex weights, and computes the residual vector-field root-mean-square magnitude in **512-canvas pixels**. The same orthogonal decomposition is applied to the retained-minus-frozen vertex increment. The square grid makes (1,x-1/2,y-1/2) mutually orthogonal, so the projection coefficients are directly computed by their dot products rather than an unstable large least-squares call. It is a geometric complexity diagnostic, not an accuracy metric, and 20 directions still come from only one known specimen.

Across those 20 directions, the equal-direction mean RMS departure from each map's best affine fit was **3.102 canvas px** for frozen and **3.324 canvas px** for retained. The learned retained-minus-frozen increment had equal-direction mean total RMS magnitude **1.486 canvas px**. Its affine projection had mean RMS **0.759 px**, while its orthogonal non-affine part had mean RMS **1.263 px**. The **mean within-direction fraction of squared vertex-displacement energy** in the non-affine part was **74.21%** (median **76.38%**, individual direction range approximately **54%–93%**). This is not strain or elastic energy. These are means of casewise magnitudes/fractions; ratios of the displayed means need not reproduce the energy fraction. A synthetic affine-plus-nonlinear unit test checked exact affine recovery and Pythagorean orthogonality. Thus the learned residual is not merely a global affine adjustment at the vertex-table level. This does **not** show that its non-affine component is anatomically correct: the seven/20 TREs remain reused-development evidence, and cycle consistency actually worsened versus affine (§5.27). Full rows are in `phase6_lung_all20_nonaffine.json` on D:.

### 5.30 Does the image proxy rank the anatomically better safe map?

The two already frozen six-channel and 12-channel models offer a particularly sharp *post-hoc* test of surrogate validity on the same 20 known-specimen directions. `tools/digital_lung_proxy_anatomy_alignment.py` joined their **pre-existing** image-proxy prediction reports and manually scored TRE reports; it did not rerun inference or open raw landmark files. For **every** direction, the external affine metadata and frozen composite 257² P1 vertex table were **bitwise identical** across model directories, and their affine/frozen anatomy means agreed. The two dynamic models used the same production MIND-like image-proxy evaluator and the same 80 annotation correspondences per direction. Thus the difference is the trained six- versus 12-channel residual, not a different start or affine frame. The 20 directions still share one previously viewed specimen, and this analysis must **not** be used to select a new checkpoint.

| Twelve-channel compared with six-channel | TRE improves | TRE worsens |
|---|---:|---:|
| Image proxy improves | 4 | 0 |
| Image proxy worsens | **13** | 3 |

The 12-channel model had lower native moving-JPEG-pixel TRE on **17/20** directions even though its dimensionless image loss was **higher on 16/20**. Across directions, mean 12-minus-six changes were **+0.0007714** image-proxy units and **−0.195585 native pixels** TRE. The magnitude of the image delta is small relative to the absolute proxy (~0.2–0.27), so this does not show a clinically large image-score failure. But the **sign disagreement on 13/20** paired directions is concrete evidence that an image-proxy validation gate would have rejected many anatomy-improving residuals on this known specimen. It also explains why the 63/18 image-proxy train/validation agreement (§5.29) cannot settle anatomical learning. This is not a patient-independent statistical correlation estimate, nor does it license tuning to the 20 reused labels. Complete rows are `phase6_lung_proxy_anatomy_alignment20.json` on D:.

For context, we evaluated **both dynamic models'** saved maps against the **same** 12-channel input match archive in each direction, using their raw aligned vertex tables before the common post-affine. The robust selected-match loss is the exact training quantity \(K^{-1}\sum_k(\sqrt{\|512[u(s_k)-t_k]\|^2+4^2}-4)\), in 512-canvas-pixel units. Match-archive affines were identical to both model archives in all 20 directions. The 12-channel model reduced this machine-match loss on **20/20** directions (equal-direction mean change **−0.39946 canvas px**), including the **three** directions where anatomy TRE worsened. Thus machine-match fit is more often sign-aligned with TRE here than the MIND-like image proxy, but it is **not an independent test**: these selected matches were a direct *input* to the 12-channel network and may include outliers. Nor can the dimensionless image delta, canvas-pixel robust-match delta, and native-JPEG-pixel TRE delta be compared by magnitude. Full match-augmented rows are in `phase6_lung_proxy_anatomy_alignment20_v2.json` on D:.

### 5.31 What forces does the four-term training loss apply to output vertices?

Loss coefficient sizes alone are misleading: the retained training weights were `(1000,0.1,0.01,0.2)` for teacher-map MSE, MIND-like image, robust selected-machine-match, and strain, but each unweighted term has a different gradient scale and support. On each of the **18 unchanged 257² validation maps**, we separately differentiated all four scalar losses with respect to the same **255² interior aligned vertex positions**, multiplied each gradient by its actual coefficient, and measured its Euclidean norm. The 25-step pseudo-teacher map's affine metadata agreed with the saved map on all 18 IDs, and the image/match scalar values reproduced the production path. No manual anatomy was read. This is an *output-space* first-order diagnostic at already trained maps; the neural-parameter gradient would additionally multiply each component by the network's Jacobian, and these norms do not describe every point of the training trajectory.

| Weighted term at saved output | Mean interior-vertex gradient norm across 18 cases | Cases largest / 18 |
|---|---:|---:|
| `1000 × teacher-map MSE` | 0.01949 | 0 |
| `0.1 × image proxy` | 0.01511 | 0 |
| `0.01 × robust machine-match loss` | **0.20577** | **18** |
| `0.2 × strain` | 0.02403 | 0 |

For every case, the match norm exceeded the largest of the other three by at least **4.80×**; the mean ratio was **8.61×**. The mean norm of the *sum* of all four weighted vectors was **0.21022**, not the sum of the displayed norms, because their directions differ. The match loss is spatially sparse (§5.26), so its large vertex-gradient norm can coexist with a small affected region and does **not** prove its influence on CNN parameter updates or that it caused the 12-channel anatomy improvement. The vertex directions here are **not restricted to the tangent of the safe decoder**; subsequent multiplication by safety/network Jacobians and minibatch aggregation may change the ranking. It does establish that at deployed maps the nominally small `0.01` match weight does **not** make the output-coordinate first-order match force negligible. An independent checker derived the map/strain/match gradients separately for three IDs and agreed with the reported norms to at most \(2.21\times10^{-7}\), while noting that the older ACROBAT match NPZs lack embedded affine metadata. The reproducible per-case file is `phase6_loss_gradient_budget_val18.json` on D:, generated by `tools/digital_proxy_gradient_conflict.py --teachers`.

### 5.32 One gradient-scale-guided weight change: lower match weight did not improve anatomy

Section 5.31 suggested that the nominal `0.01` match coefficient supplies about ten times the weighted **output-coordinate** gradient norm of the other terms at the saved validation maps. We therefore predeclared **one** ablation, changing *only* the training match coefficient to `0.001`, leaving the 12-channel dynamic-force architecture, frozen 257² starts, one-head initialization, 63/18 eligible split, seed, 25-step pseudo-teachers, 1000 minibatches of two and other coefficients `(map,image,strain)=(1000,0.1,0.2)` unchanged. It trained for **204.14 s** on an idle GPU with peak PyTorch allocation **4.655 GB**. Its entire 18-row *initial* validation report equaled the retained `0.01` control exactly, establishing an identical model/feature start before optimization. No anatomy label entered training or map prediction. This is one heuristic weight test, not a search for a best coefficient.

| 18 eligible internal-validation means | Retained `match=.01` | New `match=.001` |
|---|---:|---:|
| Pseudo-teacher map RMSE, unit square | **0.00250446** | 0.00252681 |
| MIND-like image proxy | **0.20449686** | 0.20461819 |
| Robust selected-match loss, 512-canvas px | **0.53880084** | 0.61604678 |
| Strain | 0.01469131 | **0.01360826** |

Thus the new weight lowered strain but worsened all three other mean internal-validation diagnostics. Following the predeclared protocol **despite** that proxy result, we then used the **same 20 already viewed ordered directions** of one lung specimen, reusing bitwise identical external affines, frozen maps and selected machine-match archives and retaining all directions. All 20 new maps passed saved exact Q1/boundary certificates, with finite predictor VJPs. Their minimum normalized Q1 corner was **0.091772**, versus **0.141552** for the old 20 saved maps; one new direction had **19** positive but sub-0.1 corners. Native moving-JPEG-pixel mean TRE was **8.67963 px**, versus **8.63647 px** for the retained `match=.01` model: the lower-weight model won **6/20** paired directions and lost **14/20**. Both still beat their shared initial affine and frozen map on **20/20**. The new model's mean MIND-like image proxy on these 20 directions was also worse by **0.0000541**. These correlated directions are **not** new patients; the small +0.04316-px anatomy mean difference is not a population-level estimate. The result falsifies the simple engineering rule “equalize the four output-vertex gradient norms by dividing match weight by ten, and registration will improve” for this checkpoint/data. It does not prove match loss should always dominate, because output gradients are not parameter gradients and only one weight was tested. An independent checker re-derived all 20 scores from original CSVs and saved maps to \(2.05\times10^{-14}\) px and separately confirmed all exact map certificates, common baselines/matches and configuration fields. Checkpoint/report and all saved maps/scores are on D: as `phase6_matchweight001_dynamic_strain02_1000.{pt,json}` and `phase6_matchweight001_all20/`.

### 5.33 Does the observed accuracy need every 257² control vertex?

The retained architecture *outputs* 66,049 vertices, but that alone does not prove its current registration gains require all of them. In a declared read-only compression experiment, `tools/digital_lung_nested_resolution_audit.py` took each saved **binary32 aligned 257² vertex table** for the same 20 known-specimen directions and selected every 16th/8th/4th/2nd vertex along each axis, producing nested 17²/33²/65²/129² tables. It applied the **same saved positive post-affine** and re-evaluated each new *coarse fixed-diagonal P1 interpolation* at the same 80 landmark positions per direction. Every coarse table received its **own filtered exact-sign Q1/boundary certificate**; we did not assume topology follows from the fine certificate or repair an invalid table. Temporary files used for the certificate were created and removed within the D-drive artifact directory, while the compact per-direction result was retained. This is not an exact representation of the fine P1 function and not a trained coarse neural decoder.

### 5.34 Are anatomical gains spatially confined near the selected machine matches?

The preceding output-gradient test found sparse *match-loss derivative support*, but this alone does not imply that the four-pass network only improves anatomy close to a match source: a shared convolutional network and repeated image-force updates can influence remote vertices. To test the simplest spatial-confinement hypothesis without fitting any new model, `tools/digital_lung_match_coverage_audit.py` took the **unchanged** retained maps, frozen starts, selected match archives and original manual landmarks for all 20 already viewed directions. For each fixed landmark it converted the source annotation to the fixed 512-canvas coordinate system, computed Euclidean distance to the nearest selected **fixed-side match source** in canvas pixels, and scored frozen-minus-retained P1 TRE in native moving 5%-JPEG pixels. Positive gain means the learned residual improves that landmark. The same 80 landmark identities recur across directions; this is a retrospective, correlated one-specimen diagnostic, not a test on new patients or a causal perturbation of matches.

Across 1,600 direction-landmark observations, nearest-match distance quartile edges were **0.182, 5.951, 14.488, 27.850, 173.130 canvas px**. Equal-sized pooled quartiles (400 observations each, near to far) had mean TRE gain **0.919, 0.713, 0.705, 0.698 native px**. Within each direction, its nearest half gained **0.782 px** on average and its farther half **0.735 px**; the near half won only **11/20** directions. Mean within-direction Spearman correlation between distance and gain was **−0.0141**. The all-point mean gain **0.75865 px** equals the separately reported frozen-to-retained mean TRE reduction, providing a score-frame cross-check.

There is a modest pooled near-match advantage, but no stable monotone within-direction relation. Thus the specific assertion “anatomical improvement is confined to machine-match neighborhoods” is **not supported** here. This does not prove machine matches are unnecessary: they are a network input, proximity need not capture their long-range influence, and nearest-distance is correlated with tissue content and confidence. The twenty selected match counts range **42–373** per direction, and the pooled quartiles mix directions with different difficulty and coverage. Nor does it validate the current image proxy or establish unseen-specimen generalization. An independent checker recomputed all 1,600 distance/gain pairs from original CSVs and archives (maximum pointwise difference below \(3.3\times10^{-13}\) in their respective units) and confirmed that no rank ties occur. Complete direction and point rows are in D-drive `phase6_lung_match_coverage20.json`.

### 5.35 Does the learned *non-affine* residual improve anatomy?

The geometric decomposition in §5.28 established that the learned residual contains non-affine displacement, but did not show that this component helps registration. We therefore constructed a stricter **affine-only counterfactual** from the unchanged frozen and retained aligned P1 tables, without reading landmarks for the fit. For each direction, solve the ordinary least-squares problem over all 66,049 saved vertex pairs

\[
(A_*,b_*)=\arg\min_{A\in\mathbb R^{2\times2},\,b\in\mathbb R^2}
\sum_i\|A Y_i^{\rm frozen}+b-Y_i^{\rm retained}\|_2^2.
\]

The counterfactual is the **factorized** map \(x\mapsto T_{\rm external}(A_*F_{\rm frozen}(x)+b_*)\); the full learned map is \(x\mapsto T_{\rm external}(F_{\rm retained}(x))\). Both use the *same* external image-derived positive affine \(T_{\rm external}\). Every fitted \(\det A_*\) was positive (minimum **0.99207**), so the counterfactual is itself a P1 homeomorphism as a composition of an independently certified frozen P1 map and positive affine factors. We do **not** claim a separately rounded, flattened counterfactual vertex archive has its own binary certificate. The fit is optimal in squared **vertex displacement**, not in annotation TRE, and it may move the aligned rectangle boundary before the common external affine. Importantly, this is an **affine correction to a pre-existing non-affine frozen map**, not a globally affine map. Its parameters are fitted to the *already computed retained map*, so it is a diagnostic counterfactual and **not an independently inexpensive inference baseline**.

On the same 20 already viewed, correlated directions, equal-direction mean native moving-JPEG TRE was **9.39513 px frozen → 9.26721 px affine-corrected frozen → 8.63647 px full learned P1**. The affine correction beat frozen on **14/20** directions, but full learned P1 beat this affine-corrected frozen map on **20/20** directions, by **0.054–0.943 native px** direction-wise. The full map's extra mean improvement over the correction was **0.63073 native px**, versus its total **0.75865 px** improvement over frozen. The affine fit's mean residual was **1.26048 512-canvas px**, consistent with §5.28's non-affine increment magnitude. Thus the observed *development-specimen landmark* gain is not merely another affine correction to the frozen map; useful local P1 variation contributed under this no-label-fitted vertex-L2 comparator. This is stronger than an energy decomposition but still **not an independent-patient result or a proof that no other label-free affine correction could match the annotations**. An independent checker recomputed all twenty least-squares fits by a separate centered-covariance formula and every TRE from original CSVs (maximum score difference \(1.3\times10^{-13}\) px); it checked the extra affine determinants' exact binary64 signs. Full direction rows and positive determinants are in D-drive `phase6_lung_affine_counterfactual20.json`.

## 6. Forward/VJP cost, memory and what the timer excludes

All reported layer times are GPU measurements for batch one at 257² control/output resolution. The one-shot global full-grid student needed about 48 ms forward; its forward-plus-parameter-and-selected-match VJP was about 150--290 ms depending on the pair/run, with 159 MiB forward and 1.18 GiB VJP peak `torch.cuda.max_memory_allocated` for Cc10. The one-shot 17/33/65 latent student needed about 46--48 ms forward and about 150--287 ms forward-plus-VJP, with 150 MiB forward and 784 MiB VJP peak for Cc10. Its lower VJP allocation did **not** translate to a clear anatomical accuracy gain.

The 25-step per-pair multilevel fit required 1.66--2.09 s across the seven pairs; 100 steps required 6.68--7.22 s. The 25-step Cc10 PyTorch peak allocation was 395 MiB. This makes it substantially faster than the earlier full-grid, 300-step, four-pass fit (18.9--21.8 s/pair, approximately 389 MiB PyTorch peak), but it remains a test-time iterative method, not a 50-ms neural layer. A 10-step fit took roughly 0.68--0.96 s, but did not consistently match the 25-step anatomy. The fitting timer starts after input loading, affine prewarp and descriptor creation; the one-shot layer timer includes its feature computation from decoded tensors, but not image decoding, external matcher/RANSAC, saving or exact post-save certification. PyTorch allocated-memory peaks are not total GPU residency and exclude external matcher memory. A separate Cc10 direct affine matcher/RANSAC measurement was about 0.56 s plus approximately 0.60 s one-time model loading; the additional aligned machine-match process is also not included in the decoder numbers.

For the dual ridge layer, after the initial warm-up outlier, the measured GPU forward was about 29--31 ms per pair and its backward alone about 45--54 ms, including the N-by-N solve and safe steering but not external correspondence generation, file I/O or saved-map certification. These **must be added** for a forward-plus-VJP cost of roughly 75--85 ms; reporting the backward alone as the whole gradient cost would be misleading. The one first-call Cc10 measurement was 231 ms forward/163 ms backward due to initialization effects, so this is not a cold-start latency claim. For the retained 12-channel model, §5.22 separately measures the residual-matcher, complete predictor-call and inner-neural costs on 20 directions; those stage definitions must not be interchanged.

## 7. Independent checks and current interpretation

An independent checker recomputed all 14 cold multilevel25/100 anatomy scores directly from raw landmark CSVs and saved maps (maximum discrepancy below \(5.4\times10^{-14}\) px), rechecked exact saved-binary Q1 signs and fixed boundaries on every map, and confirmed positive, bitwise-matched post-affines. All 14 maps had 262,144/262,144 positive Q1 corners. Later independent checks separately recomputed the seven dynamic-force, seven strain-0.2, seven no-match-loss, 20 ordered same-specimen cross-stain direction and 14 matched static/dynamic scores from raw annotations; §5 records their exact scope and discrepancies. The seven ridge-0.1 maps received the separate independent check described in §5.3. A direct numerical finite-difference test agreed with the low-dimensional neural decoder's analytic VJP to within 3% on a representative 257² direction; this is useful evidence but not a proof of correct gradients at all non-smooth safety boundaries. The teacher generation and seven-pair optimizer paths read images, image-derived matches and saved image-only baselines; they do not read anatomy or DHR full fields. The capacity-only ACROBAT teacher-map fit is explicitly separate from anatomical evaluation.

The evidence points to **stale or misdirected image evidence and imperfect supervision**, rather than merely inadequate vertex-grid capacity. Recomputing image force at each pass improved the six-channel model; adding image-derived residual-match features yielded the best currently retained 12-channel model, with 20/20 same-specimen directions better than their frozen starts and 17/20 better than the six-channel model. The 18 saved validation maps and eight saved confirmation maps establish topology and image-proxy behavior at actual deployment resolution, **not** anatomical generalization. Training three times longer, removing only the teacher-map gradient, adding modest monotone grayscale augmentation, or exactly conjugating four spatial symmetries did not coherently improve validation. A one-pass analytic machine-match correction improved its own match proxy on 26/26 pairs but worsened the image proxy on the same 26/26; it was not promoted to an anatomy result. A post-hoc spatial audit did not identify fixed rectangle boundary as the dominant residual-error cause, though the available labels barely sample that boundary. These conflicts make proxy design and true cross-patient evidence the main bottlenecks. The task is **not finished**: no sealed independent-patient anatomy test has been obtained, external matcher/affine estimation is nondifferentiable and excluded from layer timings, and proxy gains cannot be equated with registration accuracy. The next decisive test is a frozen-checkpoint, predeclared external anatomy cohort with image/match selection kept wholly label-independent, rather than another best-of-seven score on reused labels.

An independent checker also caught and corrected an initially invalid right-vector argument for the readout's smallest singular value: a 255×256 matrix has a right kernel, so only the final **left-vector** witness in the formulation justifies the positive-singular-value bound. The checker independently reconstructed the 18-case projection ratios from original teacher/frozen maps (maximum JSON discrepancy about \(1.1\times10^{-15}\)), checked the added head's zero-output initialization on a four-pass 12-channel U-Net, and recomputed the 20×80 pointwise native-pixel TREs (maximum discrepancy about \(2.1\times10^{-14}\) px). These checks do not create new independent anatomy cases.

For the later diagnostics, the checker reproduced all 26 image/match-gradient signs and feedback finite changes from saved maps, checked the support-restricted cosine identity, and found that a CPU gain-one feedback replay differs from the archived GPU map by at most one binary32 unit in the last place. The checker also independently implemented P1-plus-affine two-way composition and recomputed the 20 cycle-error means/medians to within \(1.25\times10^{-13}\) canvas pixels, with identical common-field-of-view counts. In particular, the cycle comparison uses three forward-intermediate masks in common but does **not** require the reverse result to remain inside the canvas; opposite directions generally test different physical query locations. These are independent numerical checks, not a theorem of anatomical validity.

For §§5.28–5.30, the checker independently fitted the 20 saved map pairs with NumPy least squares and found at most \(7.8\times10^{-15}\) difference from the regular-grid analytic projection; the reported 74.21% is a **mean of per-direction vertex-squared displacement fractions**, not the pooled energy fraction (about 71.12%). The 63 new train and 18 validation report IDs exactly matched the frozen checkpoint's respective ID lists, were disjoint, and all 81 saved maps had valid exact binary certificates; these are 63/18 *eligible* cases from a selected 102, not the full 81/21 split. The checker recomputed all train/validation image means and the 20-direction image/TRE sign table. An independent NumPy P1 interpolation of the common selected machine matches reproduced each robust-match loss to \(4.44\times10^{-16}\); the match archive is the 12-channel model's input, not independent correspondence truth. These audits support the arithmetic and frame of the diagnostic, not population generalization or a causal effect of adding channels.
