## HistoReg CD68→CD4 development-pair pixel protocol (2026-09-29)

`tools/digital_wsi_pair.py` takes only the two images and saves a moving→fixed 3×3 affine acting on column `[x,y,1]` in continuous pixel coordinates (top-left origin, x right, y down). `tools/digital_wsi_pair_eval.py` separately reads both landmark CSVs, checks their stain suffixes against the image roles, joins by identifier, checks each point against its own image dimensions, recomputes the affine determinant, and evaluates Euclidean target registration error (TRE) in **pixels**. It counts mapped points outside the fixed image instead of dropping them. The registration entry point has no landmark-file argument and does not read landmarks.

The local author example has CD68 moving (`7470×9890` pixels) and CD4 fixed (`7171×9916` pixels). Both CSVs use ` ,X,Y`, with matching IDs 1–77 and fractional pixel coordinates; all 77 input landmarks are in their respective images. These are the **provided JPEG resolutions**; their original scanner/WSI pyramid level is unknown. Pixel spacing and physical units are unavailable, so these numbers are neither micrometer TRE nor official ANHIR rTRE. Patient, tissue block, physical slide, and ANHIR set IDs are unknown. This is one **development pair**, with no group-held-out test inference or SOTA claim. The independent protocol checker also showed that bounds and ID matching cannot detect an otherwise plausible uniform CSV coordinate rescaling; an authoritative image-to-landmark scale manifest is required for formal evaluation.

| Image-only affine baseline | Matrix translation (px) | det | Inside fixed / 77 | Mean TRE (px) | Median | P95 | Max |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Identity in pixel coordinates | `(0, 0)` | 1 | 77 | 513.207 | 559.502 | 765.843 | 884.363 |
| Grayscale tissue-centroid translation, threshold `<230`, thumbnail ≤384×384 | `(+107.829, −43.065)` | 1 | 77 | 587.822 | 659.591 | 876.141 | 997.860 |

The second method uses only the images; its poorer TRE is a negative result, not a landmark-tuned choice. Positive affine determinant establishes bijectivity on the full plane for these two affine maps, but does not imply the transformed source rectangle lies wholly inside the fixed rectangle or that a later sampled nonrigid map meets the project's four-corner Q1 certificate. These baselines contain no nonrigid alignment, warped WSI export, external strong baseline, or physical-unit evaluation.

### Proven affine-head coverage limitation

The present `PositiveAffineHead` builds

$$M=\begin{pmatrix}s_x&s_xu\\ \ell s_x&\ell s_xu+s_y\end{pmatrix},\qquad s_x,s_y>0.$$

Hence every finite output has $M_{11}=s_x>0$ and $\det M=s_xs_y>0$. The safe residual map $f$ fixes the square boundary exactly, so the composite $A\circ f$ maps the bottom-edge corner difference $(1,0)-(0,0)$ to $M(1,0)$; no interior F1/F2 latent can reverse its $x$ component. In the **development** case 733, a least-squares positive affine fitted to DHR's image-only field had $M_{11}=-.843961$ and determinant `.737669`; this particular teacher boundary behavior is outside the current head's range. On the **new physical-scale canvases**, the analogous fitted positive-determinant affine has negative $M_{11}$ in three of ten cases: 586 (`−1.012809`), 638 (`−.968619`) and 733 (`−.964664`); the other seven are positive. See [the factor CSV](acrobat_10case_safe_distill.csv). This does not establish that DHR chose the anatomically correct rotation, nor that ACROBAT truth necessarily needs it. It establishes an algebraic capacity gap relative to these external teacher boundary motions. A candidate *not yet implemented* is $M=R(4\arctan t)L_{\rm tri}\operatorname{diag}(s_x,s_y)U_{\rm tri}$, where the last two factors are triangular with diagonal one. This keeps positive determinant and represents a finite 180° rotation at $t=1$; any bounded scale/shear/translation range still needs explicit coverage testing.
