# Image-only safe residual optimization after an external affine initializer

## Predeclared research card

**Question.** Does a strong, image-derived positive-orientation affine initializer make per-pair image-only optimization of a safe Q1 residual useful on the three already inspected real cross-stain pathology pairs?

**Exact claim under test.** A fixed affine postcomposition \(A(u)=Mu+b\), \(\det M>0\), followed by the existing 17² F2 seed and 33²–257² F1 residual decoder \(U_z\), yields the represented map \(\Phi_z=A\circ U_z\). The safe residual is homeomorphic on the unit square for finite latents; therefore the exact factorized map is homeomorphic onto the affine image of the square. Whether unsupervised image optimization improves appearance and landmark accuracy over \(A\circ\mathrm{id}\) is empirical, not implied by topology. The DHR initializer cost must be counted separately from Q1 optimization.

**Assumptions.** Source and target JPEGs are independently resized to the same unit-square thumbnail frame; pixel-center sampling uses `align_corners=False`. Each fixed affine factor comes from the image-only *initial-only* DHR run and has positive determinant; no landmark file enters optimization or factor selection. The three pairs and landmarks have been inspected already, so all TRE measurements are development diagnostics, not unbiased generalization.

**What would falsify utility.** A lower photometric loss without better landmark TRE, worse TRE than the fixed affine, nonfinite gradients, failed stored-binary residual/affine certificate, excessive runtime, or trivial improvement compared with an equally budgeted zero-start optimization. One pair does not establish general utility.

**Smallest decisive test.** First unit-test that `A∘U` is used in the correct sampling order and that an identity residual reproduces the fixed affine exactly, including stored factor keys. Then run a short same-budget comparison of `global_ncc` and `local_ncc` from the fixed image-only affine on the BIRL lesion pair at 257² control vertices and 512² image pixels. If executable, repeat on HistoReg and kidney. Record first/best/last image loss, complete step time, CUDA peak memory, finite VJPs, saved-field exact topology certificate, and read-only landmark TRE. Compare with the existing zero-start O mode, affine-only, and external DHR full registration without equating their runtime accounting. Do not pick a photometric loss by looking at landmark labels and then describe that choice as held-out.

**Prior work / novelty boundary.** Affine initialization followed by intensity-driven deformable registration is standard. The question here is only whether the specific forward safe Q1 latent layer can make that refinement effective and practical while preserving the exact represented homeomorphism. It is not a learned amortized image-to-map network, a direct competitor to DHR's nonrigid pipeline, or evidence of out-of-sample registration.

## Implementation and experimental conditions

The affine factor is read from each previously saved, image-only DHR *initial-only* archive `q1_dhr_initial_affine_{histo,lesions,kidney}_257_float32.npz`. The new optional `--initial-affine-map` path leaves the old zero-start behavior unchanged. The optimizer initializes all residual latents to zero and computes \(U_z\) by the unchanged safe decoder. For every fixed-image pixel center \(q\), it evaluates the Q1 residual and samples the moving image at \(A(U_z(q))\). Since \(A\) is affine, applying it to the Q1 vertex table before Q1 interpolation yields the same function in exact arithmetic. The loss is either global `1−NCC` or the code's seven-pixel-window squared local NCC loss, plus `0.05` times the mean **per coordinate** of \((U_z(v)-v)^2\), equivalently `0.025 mean_vertices ||U_z(v)−v||²`. Adam optimizes only residual latent fields; the external affine is fixed. The returned and saved object is the **factorization** \((U_z,M,b)\), not a newly rounded composite vertex table.

The runs used the three already inspected development pairs and their original JPEGs, converted to grayscale and independently resized to 512×512. Control side was 257 (66,049 Q1 vertices, 65,536 cells); batch 1, float32, 100 Adam steps at learning rate `.04` for **each** of two losses and three pairs. The GPU was an idle RTX A6000 on the AI host: global/local lesion used physical GPU0/GPU2; global/local HistoReg used GPU0/GPU2; global/local kidney used GPU6/GPU7. The locally isolated DHR *initial-only* match was performed earlier, not inside the timed optimizer loop. No landmark CSV was copied into the remote optimizer invocation. Once maps and reports were copied back to D:, the separate CPU evaluator read public development landmarks, inverted the saved factorized Q1 map, and scored Euclidean error in original fixed-JPEG pixel coordinates. HistoReg/lesion require all matching IDs; kidney uses the declared 69-ID intersection, excluding its two fixed-only labels. All large maps, full reports and evaluator JSONs are at `D:\QC_optimization_data\digital_topology_wsi\h_affine_{histo,lesions,kidney}_{global_ncc,local_ncc}100_{map.npz,report.json,eval.json}`. A ten-step lesion global-NCC smoke run preceded the six reported full runs and is not used in the comparison.

All six runs had 100/100 finite latent VJPs and returned residuals with exactly ordered identity boundaries. All six saved residual vertex tables passed the integer/binary-rational corner-sign test: 262,144/262,144 strictly positive corners each. The separately saved affine matrices also have exactly positive determinants when their binary32 entries are interpreted as rationals, so each **stored factorization** represents a Q1 homeomorphism onto its affine parallelogram. All six evaluator runs recovered one inverse root per corresponding landmark (77/77, 78/78, 69/69, respectively). The per-run CUDA peak allocated memory was 111,631,872 bytes for global NCC and 118,971,392 bytes for local NCC; this measures allocated PyTorch GPU memory, not reserved VRAM or peak of the external DHR initializer. Median step times exclude JPEG loading, process startup, output saving/certification, evaluator time and DHR initial matching. Approximate optimizer loop time is 7–8 s per pair/loss plus those excluded costs; **this is not a fast network inference number**.

**Decision from this test.** Both objectives consistently improve their own appearance score while worsening landmark TRE on all three inspected pairs. This is an empirical failure of these two specific image-only 100-step protocols, not a failure of the topological decoder or a proof no image objective can work. The saved checkpoint is selected by *appearance term alone*, although Adam follows appearance plus regularization; the border-extended moving image has no explicit overlap mask. These choices may contribute to the negative result and need separate controls. An equal-budget zero-start run and a regularization-strength ablation were not performed here, so an initializer-versus-zero-start causal comparison is **not** established. It argues against simply increasing the number of safe-layer optimization steps under these exact losses. A next discriminating test should compare tissue-structure or feature correspondence objectives, possibly with regularization/early stopping selected without development landmarks, against a nonlearned optimizer using the same safe decoder. A learned image-to-latent frontend still requires independent training groups and an out-of-sample evaluation; this H-mode experiment provides neither.

## Independent saved-map review

The code uses the correct **`A∘U`** order: Q1 weights sum to one, so affine transformation of the vertices equals affine postcomposition in exact arithmetic. Geometry uses endpoint-spaced control nodes; image-coordinate conversions use `(pixel+.5)/size` and `unit*size-.5`. The affine factors in all six saved maps are bitwise unchanged from the stated initial archives; only residual latents are optimized, and the optimization path reads no landmark files.

I independently converted each residual's binary32 coordinates to exact common-denominator integers and checked all **262,144** corner determinants: all are strictly positive, and each boundary equals the ordered unit-square identity exactly. Exact rational affine determinants are positive (approximately `.9128317123/.9988918258/1.0274906209` for HistoReg/lesion/kidney). These facts certify the **stored factorization** onto its affine parallelogram, not every rounded materialization or a map onto the moving-image square. Minimum normalized stored residual determinants range `.0499502583–.0499756525`; topology passes, but an exact stored `.05` floor would not.

For every label in all six maps, I first solved the separate affine inverse, then selected convex residual cells and inverted their bilinear formula with SciPy, without calling the production evaluator. Global/local mean TREs are **`97.1656493581/147.5371397583`**, **`12.7513429114/15.9901277032`**, and **`15.1001450237/20.5920954428`**. All **77/77, 78/78, 69/69** labels have one root; no missing inverses were dropped. The largest per-label discrepancy from the reports is `3.76e-12` px. Independently computed affine-only means are `46.3479377514/7.6650266525/10.3526967474`, confirming deterioration in all six refinements.

Deliberate attacks distinguish the necessary checks: reversing affine/residual composition on a noncommuting analytic fixture changes the answer by `.006029`; a negative-determinant affine is rejected; moving a 3×3 residual's center to `(1.5,.5)` produces four nonpositive corners despite an unchanged identity boundary. All three optimizer tests pass. Review identified the factor-of-two regularizer notation error, now corrected above. The displacement penalty is not a spatial smoothness penalty, and appearance-only checkpoint selection considers pre-update iterates, leaving the final post-update map unscored. The missing matched zero-start, regularization and overlap-mask controls prevent attributing the failure to any one component. No GPU job or production-code edit was performed for this review.
