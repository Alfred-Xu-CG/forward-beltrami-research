# Image-match calibrated forward gain for safe fine-level innovations

## Predeclared research card

**Question.** A fixed five-level residual proposal improves an exact-match synthetic map but worsens all three sparse pathology development pairs relative to the matched-width coarse decoder. Can an image-match-only, differentiable scalar gain at each level suppress nonpredictive updates without anatomical labels or a line search over completed maps?

**Exact construction.** Split accepted post-RANSAC machine correspondences deterministically into a proposal subset \(T\) (80%) and a calibration subset \(C\) (20%), seed 290929, as in the earlier sparse-match experiments. At level \(l\), form the Gaussian residual field \(R_l\) from \(T\) exactly as in `H_RESIDUAL_MULTISCALE.md`. For each \(c\in C\), let \(e_c=p_c-U_l(q_c)\) and \(r_c=R_l(q_c)\), where \(U_l\) is the current safe Q1 map. Choose
\[
 g_l=\operatorname{clip}_{[0,1]}
 \frac{\sum_{c\in C} e_c\cdot r_c}
 {\sum_{c\in C}\|r_c\|^2+10^{-8}}.
\]
This is the constrained one-variable minimizer of the stated **continuous-Gaussian surrogate** calibration objective \(\sum_c\|e_c-g r_c\|^2+10^{-8}g^2\). The code evaluates \(r_c=R_l(q_c)\) directly from the Gaussian formula; it does **not** interpolate the nodal values \(R_l(v)\) with Q1 at \(q_c\). Therefore this scalar is not generally the exact optimizer for the actual nodal Q1 map even *before* safety scaling, and certainly not for the post-F1 map. Propose \(U_l(v)+g_l R_l(v)\), apply the unchanged F1-D hard safety layer, then refine. Use the already frozen widths \((.12,.06,.03,.015,.01)\) and update sides 17²–257². The gain itself is a piecewise differentiable function of match coordinates and displacements (except clamp ties and Q1 cell boundaries), not a trainable parameter in this experiment. No anatomical label, hidden test set, or per-pair map optimization enters it.

**Topology and falsifier.** The pre-safety gain neither proves nor harms topology; F1-D still supplies the Q1/P1 guarantee and saved maps need exact-binary checks. The approach fails as a practical remedy if it does not improve the same-width fixed-gain five-level synthetic or real-pair anatomy, chooses arbitrary gains unstable to the match split, or costs too much relative to fixed gain. Calibration matches are *used for inference* and can no longer be called held-out evaluation; use the analytic off-grid target and anatomical landmarks as separate post-hoc diagnostics only, not for gain choice. Post-RANSAC leakage is unchanged.

**Smallest decisive test.** Unit-test zero/negative/positive gain cases, exact formula versus a one-dimensional brute-force calibration objective, finite match-coordinate VJP away from Q1 vertices, and safe output. Run the same 257² analytic sinusoid but split its 256 exact matches 80/20; compare fixed gain 1 versus calibrated gain at identical widths on all-vertex and 10,000 seeded off-grid errors, with every \(g_l\) reported. Run the three frozen real match sets with no retuning; report calibration-set residual before/after (explicitly not a generalization metric), original-JPEG Q1/P1 TRE, stored signs, forward/VJP and allocated memory. Compare specifically against both same-width coarse and five-level fixed-gain controls; do not select a best case by labels.

**Prior work and novelty boundary.** This is elementary least-squares scalar shrinkage/cross-fitting of image-generated proposals, not new optimization mathematics. The research question is whether it integrates usefully with exact same-table multiscale hard-topology decoding at dense control resolution. Even success would be a feature-to-map mechanism with an expensive external matcher, not G2 learned end-to-end image inference.

## First frozen comparison

The scalar formula was checked in a double-precision unit test against its direct numerator/denominator calculation, together with zero/opposed-motion calibration fixtures, valid Q1 signs, identity boundary and finite source/calibration coordinate gradients. In full runs, the proposal set retains the fixed 80% split and the other 20% is **consumed by gain inference**. Consequently its final match error is a calibration-objective diagnostic and must not be called held-out accuracy. Output maps use the same saved float32 Q1 residual plus positive-affine factorization; every one of the following maps passed the exact-binary corner/boundary/affine audit, and the fixed SW–NE P1 view has positive triangles.

On the analytic sinusoidal 257² target with 204 proposal matches and 52 calibration matches, the fixed-gain and calibrated runs used exactly the same accepted split, widths and 10,000 seed-290929 analytic off-grid queries. The target has continuum Jacobian lower bound `.497345` and sampled Q1 positivity. All gains/gradients were finite. The calibrated rule gave gains **`(0,0,0)`** for constant-width coarse, **`(0,.0481,1)`** for matched-width coarse, and **`(0,.0481,1,1,1)`** for five levels. The zero first gain is not a coding failure: a wide `.12` smoothing kernel can produce a field anti-correlated with this alternating high-frequency target on the separate calibration points. ## Independent check

Independent CPU review used separate NumPy interpolation, SciPy Q1 inversion, triangle-barycentric P1 inversion, and exact stored-coordinate arithmetic; it did not call the production certificate or rerun GPU jobs.

- **Formula and gradients.** With positive ridge, the scalar quadratic has the unique constrained minimizer shown above. An independent double-precision fixture gave gain `.4082522070971696` (dense scalar search `.40825`); a simultaneous source/target directional derivative through all four proposal/calibration inputs agreed with central differences within `3.01e-9`. The trace alone is detached, not the gain computation. CPU regressions: 12 passed, one CUDA-only test skipped. These checks establish a local VJP away from clamp and Q1-cell ties, not global smoothness or a full Jacobian check.
- **Important objective limitation, corrected during review.** Direct Gaussian evaluation at calibration points differs from Q1 interpolation of its nodal samples (an independent 17² fixture differed by `1.02e-6`). Thus the exact minimizer is for the continuous-Gaussian surrogate, not the nodal pre-safety objective or final safe map. Two deliberate boundary cases also passed: zero/opposed proposal correlation gives finite zero gain; a calibration point on the fixed boundary gave positive gain `.4082475`, yet its actual final residual stayed `.0085440`, exactly unchanged. Positive surrogate gain does not ensure a realizable correction or improved post-safety calibration error.
- **Stored geometry.** For all six synthetic and three real maps, scaling binary32 residual coordinates by `2^40` produced exact integers; arbitrary-precision integer products verified all four corner determinants per cell strictly positive, with exact identity boundary. Real minimum normalized determinants were `.8531629/.8416290/.8026572`; their stored affine factors were unchanged from initialization and had positive exact rational determinants. The guarantee concerns the factorized exact interpretation `A∘U`, onto its affine parallelogram, not onto the moving image square or an independently rounded composite array. The fixed-diagonal P1 interpretation is also safe but remains a different interior interpolant.
- **Independent accuracy reproduction.** Every real label had one inverse: HistoReg 77, lesion 78, kidney 69. Mean Q1 TRE was `25.7482379886 / 8.5557720955 / 7.5137006598` px and P1 TRE `25.7483357963 / 8.5558000409 / 7.5136838000` px; maximum per-label disagreement with the saved evaluations was `2.71e-12` px. This independently used image-center conversion `(pixel+.5)/size`, inverted the affine first, then the residual cell, and returned `unit*fixed_size-.5`. Separately regenerated synthetic vertex/off-grid errors agree with the table: calibration worsens off-grid RMSE in all three schedules; the constant-width calibrated map is exactly identity.
- **Information and reporting limits.** Calibration points are consumed by inference, not held-out evaluation. Review found stale held-back counts in all three real reports; the author corrected only metadata to held-back zero and calibration `24/30/17`, retaining an explicit correction note, which was rechecked. No anatomical labels enter gain selection. Nevertheless, calibrated inference uses an additional 20% of machine correspondences relative to the fixed-gain proposal-only control, and all cases have already been repeatedly inspected; improvement cannot establish independent generalization or isolate gain quality from the additional information. An all-match fixed-gain control and split-seed stability would address those separate questions. The conservative “suppresses fine updates, does not beat old coarse” conclusion is supported. Saved synchronized decoder timing is descriptive evidence only here; no independent timing rerun was performed.
