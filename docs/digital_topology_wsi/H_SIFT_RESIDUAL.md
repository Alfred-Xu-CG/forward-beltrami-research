# Sparse image-feature proposals for the safe Q1 residual

## Predeclared research card

**Question.** The fixed-affine image-only Q1 experiments lower intensity-correlation losses but worsen anatomical landmark accuracy in all three inspected cross-stain pairs. Can image-derived *spatial feature correspondences*, after the same DHR initial affine, supply a more useful nonrigid residual objective without surrendering the exact Q1 guarantee?

**Exact claim under test.** Let \(A\) be the fixed positive-orientation affine estimated from images and let \(J(x)=I_M(A(x))\) be the moving thumbnail resampled into the fixed normalized frame. If SIFT matches a fixed point \(q_i\) to a point \(p_i\) in \(J\), optimize the safe residual \(U_z\) against \(U_z(q_i)\approx p_i\). The represented registration map remains \(A\circ U_z\) regardless of match quality. Better landmark target registration error (TRE) than \(A\circ\mathrm{id}\) is an empirical hypothesis, not a geometric consequence.

**Assumptions.** Input images are the same two 512² grayscale JPEG thumbnails and affine archive as the prior H-mode experiment. SIFT keypoints, ratio filtering and RANSAC use images only; the evaluator-only CSVs never enter matching or optimization. The affine warp follows the same normalized half-pixel convention as the Q1 sampler. Returned SIFT matches may be wrong or spatially concentrated; therefore count, inlier ratio, spatial coverage and correspondence residual must be reported before fitting.

**What would falsify utility.** Too few reliable matches, poor field coverage, an inability to fit matches within a time budget, decreased held-back match consistency, worse landmark TRE than affine-only, an invalid stored factorization, or a total runtime that erases useful gains. Match fitting itself does not create a learned network.

**Smallest decisive test.** First implement and unit-test affine-image resampling: at pixel centers the warped moving thumbnail must agree with direct `A∘identity` Q1 image sampling. Then extract SIFT matches on the lesion pair, compare raw ratio matches and conservative RANSAC inliers; only if there are sufficient and spatially distributed matches fit a 257² safe residual from the fixed affine. Use a deterministic image-only split of accepted matches for training versus held-back match error, then evaluate landmarks separately. If viable, repeat HistoReg and kidney with unchanged hyperparameters. Report no-improvement cases; do not retune via development landmarks and claim held-out validation. The SIFT/affine extraction cost is part of the full pipeline.

**Prior work / novelty boundary.** SIFT+RANSAC and landmark-driven deformable registration are established. Here SIFT correspondences are machine-generated from input images, not anatomical ground truth. This test can reveal whether the existing safe Q1 decoder can exploit a less stain-sensitive objective; it does not meet the learned-amortized G2 criterion and cannot prove new registration accuracy on unseen pathology sets.

## First-stage extraction result (512², no fitting)

`tools/digital_q1_sift_residual.py` implements the half-pixel affine warp and a fixed conservative filter: SIFT up to 4,000 features per grayscale thumbnail, mutually consistent 0.75 nearest-neighbor ratio matches, then a partial-affine RANSAC with a six-pixel threshold. A unit test verifies that its pre-warp agrees with directly sampling the affine-postcomposed identity Q1 map at pixel centers. The affine comes unchanged from the image-only DHR initial-only archive; no labels enter this stage. The JSONs containing all retained point coordinates and timing are at `D:\QC_optimization_data\digital_topology_wsi\h_sift_{histo,lesions,kidney}_matches.json`.

The HistoReg and lesion outputs are explicitly marked `insufficient_matches`. Kidney's 12 inliers occupy only two bins and lie within x≈0.099–0.180 of the normalized canvas; fitting a whole-domain residual to that narrow cluster would not test a spatially informative nonrigid proposal. Accordingly **no Q1 latent fit or landmark TRE claim was made from these SIFT matches**. This is a negative result for this predeclared 512² conservative extraction configuration, not evidence that feature correspondence objectives in general fail. The next targeted remedies are higher-resolution and contrast-normalized extraction, or using the SuperPoint/SuperGlue feature model already present in the external DHR initializer; each requires its own image-only protocol and must include feature-extraction time.

## Predeclared resolution follow-up

Before new extraction, keep the exact detector, 0.75 mutual ratio, 6-pixel RANSAC threshold, fixed input affine and three image pairs; change only the independently resized thumbnail side from 512 to **1024**. This doubles linear spatial detail without adopting a label-tuned threshold. Report all three match counts, occupied bins and wall times. If this still lacks image-wide support, do not loosen all filters until a few matches fit; instead test the already-used SuperPoint/SuperGlue matcher on affine-aligned thumbnails as a distinct method. A match fit requires both at least eight accepted matches and support across more than the narrow two-bin kidney cluster observed above; otherwise stop at extraction.

At 1024² with all those filters unchanged, HistoReg had 379/1,130 keypoints, 5 mutual-ratio matches and 0 RANSAC inliers (1.78 s); lesion 4,002/4,000 keypoints, 9 matches and 3 inliers (2.13 s); kidney 4,000/4,001 keypoints, 6 matches and 0 inliers (1.86 s). The fixed six-pixel RANSAC threshold becomes a *smaller physical-unit radius* at 1024², but the match scarcity appears already at the pre-RANSAC ratio stage. Thus simply doubling image side did not solve the correspondence bottleneck. No Q1 fit was attempted. Full extraction JSONs are on D: as `h_sift_{histo,lesions,kidney}_1024_matches.json`.

## Predeclared learned-feature extraction follow-up

The existing DHR initializer successfully obtains a global affine using its pretrained SuperPoint/SuperGlue model. To test whether that matcher provides usable *post-affine local* correspondences, run the same model and supplied weights once on each pair of 512² grayscale thumbnails after warping the moving image with the fixed initial affine. Keep DHR's model settings (up to 3,000 SuperPoint keypoints, match threshold .3, 30 Sinkhorn iterations), a six-pixel partial-affine RANSAC filter and no landmark input. Report raw matches, inliers, four-by-four occupancy, median residual distance, model-load/match time and GPU allocation. Only attempt safe Q1 latent fitting if accepted matches cover substantially more of the image than SIFT's two-bin kidney cluster. This is an external pretrained feature method, not our learned image-to-Q1 network; any pipeline timing must count it.

The fixed 512² SuperPoint/SuperGlue extraction produced **247 raw / 148 RANSAC-inlier matches across 16/16 bins** for lesion, **148 / 116 across 14/16** for HistoReg, and **157 / 81 across 12/16** for kidney. Median post-affine matched-point distances were 3.61/2.83/5.00 thumbnail pixels. Single-command decode/align/model/match times were 1.73/3.33/2.09 s, respectively; model-build-and-load contributed .256/.368/.299 s and inference plus transfer to CPU .387/.409/.430 s. Peak allocated CUDA memory was 268.5/267.0/266.9 MB. These are different procedures and input preprocessors than DHR's original multi-scale initializer; neither the match count nor timing should be substituted for the DHR baseline. The full correspondence JSONs are on D: as `h_superglue_{lesions,histo,kidney}_512_matches.json`; no anatomy label entered the matcher.

## Predeclared safe sparse-correspondence fit

Because all three SuperGlue match sets span at least 12 of 16 bins, now fit the same safe 257² residual decoder against **machine-generated** coordinates. Use all four 17² F2 seed passes and the 33² and 65² F1 latent fields as trainable; keep 129² and 257² F1 latent fields identically zero, retaining a dense 257² output but avoiding unconstrained fine-scale fit from only 81–148 points. Hold out 20% of accepted matches with one deterministic random split (seed 290929) per pair; never use anatomical labels for the split, loss, selection or stopping. Optimize 200 Adam steps at `.04` on the remaining matches, with training loss `mean_train ||U_z(q_i)−p_i||² + .05 mean_{v,coordinate}(U_z(v)−v)²`; keep the best map by **held-back match vector RMSE**, including identity as the zero-step candidate. Report train and held-back match error, step time, GPU memory, stored exact Q1+affine certificate, then independent landmark TRE versus affine-only. Use exactly the same hyperparameters for all three pairs, even if one looks worse. This is not a learned network or an unbiased test set; match holdout points remain correlated within the same images.

## Sparse fit result

`tools/digital_q1_match_fit.py` samples the same bilinear Q1 residual at arbitrary fixed-frame match coordinates using differentiable interpolation. It never reads image data or anatomical labels; its inputs are the frozen image-generated match JSON, fixed affine archive, hyperparameters and optimizer seed. The accepted SuperGlue matches were split 80/20 by the same NumPy RNG seed in each pair **after** RANSAC had already used the whole match set; thus the held-back subset is an internal model-selection check, not a fully independent test of match generation. The residual seed and two early F1 levels were optimized; late 129² and 257² fields stayed zero. Thus the **output has 257² control vertices**, but its effective nonrigid degrees of freedom are limited to the 65² scale; it is not evidence of accurate independent 257²-detail control. Dyadic Q1 subdivision preserves the coarse Q1 function in exact arithmetic; saved binary32 fine nodes are re-audited because rounding need not preserve that equality exactly.

| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| HistoReg | 92 / 24 | `.005524→.004305` | 28 | `46.35→42.12` | 65.4 ms |
| BIRL lesion | 118 / 30 | `.010863→.008326` | 22 | `7.67→7.85` | 66.7 ms |
| BIRL kidney | 64 / 17 | `.012206→.007803` | 3 | `10.35→10.16` | 64.5 ms |

All three 200-step runs had 200/200 finite latent VJPs. In the loop, selection checks the map **before** the optimizer update, so reported best indices 28/22/3 correspond to 27/21/2 completed updates; identity was also a zero-update candidate. The selected **stored** residual tables have ordered identity boundaries and 262,144/262,144 strictly positive Q1 corner signs each; their unchanged saved affine factors have exactly positive binary-rational determinants. Each stored factorization \(A\circ U\) is therefore a homeomorphism onto its affine parallelogram under the proved Q1 hypotheses. The independent landmark evaluator recovered unique inverse roots for all 77/78/69 matching labels, respectively. Pair-specific peak PyTorch CUDA *allocated* memory was about 85 MB for the Q1 fit, excluding feature extraction. The 200-step run took about 13 s of measured complete steps even though the best held-back-match checkpoint was reached before step 30 in these three cases; external feature extraction adds roughly 1.7–3.3 s and the DHR initial-affine pipeline must also be counted. Current execution is therefore **not fast image-to-map inference**. Full map archives, optimizer reports and read-only landmark evaluator outputs are on D: as `h_superglue_fit_{histo,lesions,kidney}_257_{map.npz,report.json,eval.json}`.

The image-generated held-back match error improves on all three pairs, but anatomical TRE improves modestly on only two and worsens on lesion. Because all matched points come from the same image pair and because these are three already inspected development pairs, this is *not* evidence of cross-case generalization or general stain robustness. It does show that the external matched-feature signal obtained on **these cases** can produce a nontrivial safe residual where the two direct intensity-correlation objectives were consistently harmful. For the stated neural-layer goal, the unresolved tasks remain amortizing the image→latent inference, controlling fine-scale detail without sparse-match overfit, and validating on independent pathology groups. The currently observed small gains do not justify presenting this as a superior registration method over DHR full nonrigid registration.

## Independent sparse-feature review

The direction is correct: matching fixed `q` to `p` in `J=I_M∘A` implies `U(q)≈p` and moving query `A(U(q))`. Both match coordinates use `(pixel+.5)/size`, while residual queries use the endpoint-based Q1 grid. An independent rectangular affine-ramp fixture agrees with the pre-warp within `8.89e-16`; deliberately using the inverse affine gives `.83086` maximum intensity error. A known `(3,-2)`-pixel fixture preserves half-pixel coordinates exactly; reversing match direction gives `.056337` normalized error and dropping the half-pixel term gives `.005524` coordinate error. Four focused tests pass. Neither extraction nor fitting reads anatomical labels.

The saved JSONs independently give **116/148/81** accepted matches, **14/16/12** occupied bins and **92+24/118+30/64+17** deterministic splits (HistoReg/lesion/kidney). Direct four-weight Q1 interpolation of saved maps gives held-back RMSE **`.00430487557/.00832647227/.00780323654`**, within `9e-9` of reports. These are selection data, not test data: the same images, pretrained matching and all-match RANSAC filtering precede the split. There is no independent pathology or spatial-block holdout. Selected pre-update indices 28/22/3 correspond to 27/21/2 completed optimizer updates.

Arbitrary-precision integer determinants of the exact stored binary32 residuals are positive at all **262,144** corners per map; identity boundaries are exact. Affine factors are bitwise unchanged from the declared initial archives and have positive exact rational determinants. Separately solving the affine inverse and then candidate residual cells with SciPy reproduces **42.1200848108/7.8528167423/10.1636362085 px** mean TRE, with **77/77, 78/78, 69/69** unique roots and at most `2.02e-12` px per-label discrepancy. This certifies the stored factorization onto its affine parallelogram, not onto the moving square or every rounded composite table.

Zero late proposals give exact subdivision in ideal arithmetic, but the actual 257² binary32 tables differ from exact interpolation of their 65² subgrids by at most **`1.006e-7/1.044e-7/9.314e-8`** per component; their topology was checked directly. Held-back consistency and two small development TRE gains do not establish general stain robustness. The intensity-objective comparison also changes trainable scales and checkpoint selection, so does not isolate the objective alone. Current match/affine paths agree, but the fitter does not enforce that an arbitrary supplied affine archive matches the frame used to generate a match JSON. No GPU jobs or production edits were used for this review.
